## 0. The proposed inversion (a reframing for Michael's ratification)

> This section states a *proposed* reframing of the project's emphasis — authored for Michael, who owns the thesis framing. It is not a decision he has made. The architecture this document specifies (the `Verdict`/`Certificate` contract, the exact-rational core, the decider registry) is useful to the library regardless of which framing he chooses; the framing below is the part for him to accept, refine, or reject.

The vision essays once led with a *theorem* — soundness is independent of the selection policy — and a *scalar*, Φ_PN. The proposed reframing inverts the emphasis, and this design is written from the inverted position. The argument for the inversion is in [latent-architecture.md](../essays/latent-architecture.md) and [README.md](../essays/README.md); the premises this spec is written *against* (proposed, not yet ratified by Michael) are:

- **The certificate-and-checker is the stone** — an interoperable, machine-checkable certificate for every verdict, re-validated by a small external checker that *is* the entire trusted base (Epic C; here F3 + the original-net invariant of §5).
- **The falsifiable headline is empirical** — a measured coverage number `f_struct`: on the real MCC P/T corpus a polynomial structural certificate decides a large, characterizable fraction of queries without state-space exploration, and abstains honestly elsewhere (Epic G). Not a theorem.
- **The soundness firewall is the enabling property, not the headline.** As a theorem it is a one-line corollary of certifying algorithms composed with algorithm selection; its non-trivial content is a *precondition the code must first discharge* — every fast decider must be certifying. The two `Some(false)` stubs (§1) and the silent PNML corruptions (§1.1) violate it today. The figure of merit is the **certifying fraction `f`**, measured and required non-increasing in its trusted-base complement.
- **Learned selection is the SATzilla-style sequel** (Epic D ladder), gated behind the checker so a mis-selection costs time, never correctness. **MCC ranking is OUT** as a thesis goal: the contest is the crucible and the label source, not a leaderboard.
- **The scalar Φ_PN is dissolved.** What survives are **two computable, monotone, theorem-backed-zero, per-property factorization residuals — Φ_bound and Φ_inv** — whose deliverable is their *measurement over the corpus* (§8). IIT is absent from the repository and is not introduced.

The remainder specifies the architecture the inversion requires.

## 1. A soundness defect in the current LP-based deciders

The marking-equation deciders described elsewhere as "exact" are implemented over floating-point arithmetic. [`find_marking_equation_rational_solution`](../../petrivet/src/core/analysis/semi_decision.rs) returns `Box<[f64]>`; the integer variant rounds via `v.round() as u32`; the structural-boundedness witness is an `f64` weight vector. Each is assembled from `f64::from(incidence.get(p,t))` and solved by `microlp`'s floating-point simplex.

This has a direct consequence for soundness. A floating-point feasibility result near a constraint boundary can be incorrect, and a rounded firing-count vector does not constitute a proof. Exact arithmetic is therefore not only the prerequisite for new algebraic results (invariants, rank, Farkas duals); it is the prerequisite for the *existing* algebraic deciders to produce checkable certificates at all. The design therefore has two primary dependencies, not one: **exact arithmetic** (so that witnesses can be checked) and the **certificate calculus** (so that checking is the only trusted operation). This finding survives unchanged into the ratified plan as the soundness precondition of the whole construction.

The defect has a worse, quieter relative. The negative verdicts `Unreachable`/`Uncoverable` rest on `microlp` *failing* to find a rational solution ([`reachability.rs`](../../petrivet/src/api/system/reachability.rs) line 177). A spurious floating "infeasible" on a genuinely feasible rational system yields a silent **false `Unreachable`** — and unlike the two stubs of §3, the firewall cannot catch it: there is no positive object to check on the negative path, so the wrong answer carries no certificate to reject it. This is the floating-`Unreachable`-verdict audit (backlog B1a / M3); it is A2-priority and data-dependent, hence invisible to any single example.

### 1.1 Input fidelity at the PNML boundary is a soundness concern

Before any decider runs, the converter can already have changed the net. Initial markings above `u32::MAX` are silently clamped ([`convert.rs`](../../petrivet/src/api/pnml/convert.rs) line 287); weighted arcs are silently linearised to weight-1 (`convert.rs` lines 262–267). Each produces a *structurally different net* than the input — wrong-net-in, confident-answer-out — contrary to the converter's own stated discipline (`convert.rs` line 37). These rank, in priority, with the two stubs: a certificate that checks against the wrong net is sound about nothing. The fix is to make both cases hard `PnmlConversionError`s (or explicit, recorded caveats), so import never fabricates a different net. (Backlog A7/E4/E7; milestone M0's defect ledger and M1's regression set.)

The fix changes the return type to `Option<bool>` and returns `None` (a `const fn`): a decider that cannot certify escalates instead of fabricating. The infallible `liveness()` is retained for callers that have established decidability and now *panics* (documented) rather than fabricating — the `foo()`/`try_foo()` idiom. The full type-distinct three-valued verdict (`Verdict<P, N>` with an inhabited `Inconclusive`) is the M1 contract; A5 supplies the abstaining boundary M0 requires. No path returns `L0` as a proxy for unknown.

The measured floor (2026-06-20): structural 6, choice-class 6, not-structural 24, conversion-rejected 9; `f_struct` = **0.167** in-scope (denom 36), **0.133** all-corpus (denom 45). This is intentionally low — before any Epic-B generator only the two T-net fixtures decide structurally — and is the **starting line** against which M6–M8 are measured, never the thesis headline. A `[REGRESS]` test pins it; a fall or a corpus-size change fails. **S3:** the floor lives in `mcc-tests`, downstream of the core; `petrivet` imports nothing observer-side (`cargo tree -p petrivet -i mcc-tests` finds no edge).

**S4 repair — two engine-correctness defects uncovered closing the green gate.** The first M0 commit landed on a red lib tree: 5–6 unit tests failed (the count flickering with HashMap iteration order). The original diagnosis — an `add_arcs` chained-tuple *builder* bug — was **wrong** (doctrine #1, verify before you cut). A live-tree bisection showed `add_arcs`/`IntoArcs` produces the correct arc chain; the failures came from two deeper, genuinely-soundness-relevant defects, each fixed with a regression test:

  1. **Firing was a no-op.** [`PetriNet::fire_unchecked`](../../petrivet/src/api/system/mod.rs) computed `self.marking[p_idx].checked_sub(1)` / `.checked_add(1)` but **discarded the returned value** (`checked_*` on `u32` yields a new value; it does not mutate in place). Every public `try_fire`/`fire_unchecked`/`choose_and_fire`/`fire_any` left the marking unchanged — the simulation surface was inert, and `basic_firing`/`into_parts` failed because the token never moved. (The state-space explorer's separate `fire` in [`core/state_space/mod.rs`](../../petrivet/src/core/state_space/mod.rs) uses in-place `decrement`/`increment` and was always correct, which is why exploration-based analyses still passed and the defect hid.) Fix: assign the checked result back (`self.marking[p_idx] = self.marking[p_idx].checked_sub(1).expect(..)`), preserving the documented under/overflow panic. This is a soundness defect by the doctrine's lights — a trusted operation silently producing wrong dynamics — squarely in M0's remit.

  2. **The petgraph mirror was wired between the wrong nodes.** [`NetBuilder::build`](../../petrivet/src/api/builder.rs) populated the petgraph `Net::graph` by iterating `place_to_index.values()` / `transition_to_index.values()` to create node handles, then **addressing the resulting arrays by dense index**. `HashMap::values()` yields *hash* order, not dense-index order, so the place/transition → `NodeIndex` correspondence was permuted and the edges connected the wrong nodes. Every consumer of `Net::graph` — `circuits()`, marked-graph and state-machine liveness, `tarjan_scc`-derived strong connectivity — was silently corrupted in a hash-order-dependent (hence flaky) way; the visible symptom was a *source* transition (empty preset) reported as lying on a circuit, which is structurally impossible. Fix: build the node-handle arrays in dense-index order (`(0..ordered_places.len()).map(|p_idx| graph.add_node(IdxNode::Place(p_idx)))`), so array position equals dense index by construction. A direct `[REGRESS]` test (`builder::tests::petgraph_mirror_agrees_with_dense_adjacency`) pins the invariant at its source: the edge set of `graph` equals the dense flow relation, and no source transition appears on a circuit.

  **Two pre-existing tests carried *incorrect expectations* masked by defect (2)** and were corrected to the textbook semantics the fix restored (S1: now agree with ground truth). `liveness::t_net_source_transition_l4` left an unmarked circuit yet asserted L4 for its circuit transitions, contradicting the marked-graph liveness theorem (live ⇔ every circuit holds a token) and its own "if all circuits are marked" precondition; it now marks the circuit, and a companion `t_net_source_transition_unmarked_circuit_dead` pins the L0 (dead) case. `reachability::general_net_reachability_fallback` built a net that is actually *free-choice* (so it never reached the general fallback it claimed to test); it now uses a genuinely `General` net (two place-postsets that intersect but are incomparable, failing even asymmetric-choice). **S2:** these are corrections of known-wrong assertions and a known-wrong engine, not decisiveness regressions — no `Decided` verdict became `Inconclusive`. The lib suite is now green (121 passing, 0 failing); the remaining red items (`playground` example E0308; the absent-fixture / absent-`oracle/models` integration tests) pre-exist on `HEAD~1` (f3356bc), are missing *data/example* artifacts rather than core defects, and lie outside the M0 lib green gate.

## 2. Scope and definitions

The intended system is a pipeline of five functions: **deciders** produce candidate witnesses; **checkers** validate witnesses against the original net and thereby establish verdicts; a **selection policy** orders decider execution; an **observation layer** measures execution cost; and **compositional analysis** quantifies the degree to which a net fails to decompose. Each function depends on a domain the repository does not yet contain.

A "foundation" in this document is a set of required object types, each with the algebraic laws it must satisfy, together with one architectural decision: the boundary of the trusted computing base (TCB), and the invariant that holds it constant under everything the deciders and reductions do (§3, §5).

The recurring mathematical structures across the system are four — partial orders, ideal completions, closure operators, and quotients. The current code implements each by hand for specific cases. The foundation provides a named, exact, certificate-bearing implementation of each.

The required components are enumerated **F1–F8**, grouped into three layers (production, validation, routing/observation), one transversal component (the order abstraction), and one dependent component (compositional analysis). [`BACKLOG.md`](../../BACKLOG.md) sequences them into gated milestones under epics A–H; each F-number below is tagged with its epic.

## 3. Trust architecture

The first decision is the boundary of the trusted computing base. Soundness depends only on a small, enumerable set of components; everything else — deciders, the selection policy, the observation layer, future reductions, any learned model — may contain errors without affecting correctness.

```
TRUSTED COMPUTING BASE  (small, audited, measured, ideally formally verified)
  • certificate checkers           (F3)  — check(net, m0, query) against the ORIGINAL net
  • exact-arithmetic kernel        (F1)  — the dot-product / rank / Farkas the checkers rely on
  • engine primitives              (existing) — fire / is_enabled / the product order
  • the remaining bare-boolean deciders  — those not yet certifying (A6); the residue to shrink

OUTSIDE THE TCB  (errors cost execution time, not correctness)
  • deciders that emit a checked certificate     (F5; Epic B generators)
  • the selection policy and driver              (F5)
  • the observation crate                        (F6)
  • future certified reductions and any learned model   (Epics F, D-ladder)
```

The deliverable of the validation layer is an **interface boundary**, not a feature. If `Verdict::Proven`/`Refuted` can be constructed only by passing a certificate through its checker, the policy-independence of soundness (§5) is a type invariant rather than a runtime hope. Two enforceable rules carry it:

1. **No TCB component depends on any component outside it.** A CI lint asserts the dependency direction (`petrivet` never imports `petrivet-observe`; the producer/observer set is downstream-only).
2. **The trusted base is measured and non-increasing.** The TCB is exactly `{F3 checkers} ∪ {remaining bare-boolean deciders}`. Its bare-boolean complement is the residue A6 enumerates; CI reports its size and forbids it to grow. The certifying fraction `f` is its dual figure of merit — the share of accepted verdicts that *do* carry a checked certificate — and is reported over the corpus.

This is the GRAT discipline (Lammich): an unverified, possibly buggy *generator*; a small, eventually formally verified *checker*; trust confined to the latter. Petrivet adopts it wholesale and makes its trusted base a *measured, monotone quantity* rather than a claim.

## 4. Production layer

### F1 · Exact arithmetic and linear algebra (primary dependency)  — *Epic B0/B1a*

**Function.** A Petri net's linear theory is defined over ℤ and its field of fractions ℚ. Checkable algebraic certificates cannot be produced over `f64`. F1 supplies an exact scalar type and exact matrix operations — fraction-free (Bareiss) at the core, so that intermediate magnitudes stay polynomial.

**Types.**
- `Rational`: a normalized exact rational with an exact zero-test. Its representation and overflow policy is the first engineering decision of the program: `i64/i64` normalized is fast but overflows on large MCC instances; the alternatives are `i128` with overflow detection and promotion, or a bignum dependency (`num-rational`, currently absent from the dependency tree). This decision gates F1 and affects the cost of every downstream proof.
- `Matrix<S>` / `Vector<S>` over `S ∈ {Rational, i64}`, built on the existing dense [`IncidenceMatrix`](../../petrivet/src/core/analysis/incidence.rs) storage (currently `new`/`get` only).

**Algebra and laws.** Field axioms for `Rational`; canonical normalization; exact equality. On matrices, via a **fraction-free Bareiss elimination**: `rank`, `kernel` (right null-space basis — the T-semiflows), `left_kernel` (kernel of the transpose — the P-semiflows), `solve` (a particular solution plus the null-space), and `farkas_certificate`: on an infeasible system `Cx = b, x ≥ 0`, return an *exact* dual `y` with `yᵀC ≥ 0` and `yᵀb < 0`. **Required law: every returned object is verifiable by one exact dot product.** Smith/Hermite normal form is deferred to a scoped sub-task (B0b) used only by integer-marking refinement and minimal-semiflow extraction; SNF is the wrong default cost (see §4.1).

**Enables.**
1. Replacement of the floating-point marking-equation and boundedness deciders with exact versions, so they can emit certificates (resolves §1).
2. `rank(C)`, which with the cluster count from F4 gives the Rank Theorem (well-formed ⇔ `rank = c − 1`).
3. Exact extraction of the Farkas dual currently computed and discarded at `reachability.rs` line 177 — a P-invariant certificate for unreachability, re-checked by one dot product. The exact recheck is also what closes the silent false-`Unreachable` hole (§1; B1a): a negative verdict returns only after `y·C = 0 ∧ y·(m'−m₀) ≠ 0` holds in exact arithmetic, or after null-space membership of `m'−m₀` in `ker(Cᵀ)` is decided over ℚ.
4. Null-space bases, which are the semiflows of F2.
5. The Esparza–Melzer refinement loop (marking equation plus trap/siphon constraints), which strengthens the order-insensitive over-approximation.

**Integration point.** Replace the `f64::from(...)` LP assembly in `semi_decision.rs` with exact solves; retain `good_lp`/`microlp` only as an *inexact filter that never constructs `Proven`/`Refuted`* — it may suggest, never decide.

### 4.1 · Arithmetic regimes and algebraic rationale

The "exact arithmetic" requirement is not monolithic. Three regimes coexist, distinguished by a single discriminating principle; choosing the wrong one is either a soundness hole (too weak) or a needless cost (too strong). Recorded here so the discernment is not lost to a transcript.

**Three regimes.**

- **(a) Floating LP (`microlp`) — heuristic accelerator only.** Sound as a "maybe" filter: it may route a query to a cheaper decider or abstain, but it must **never construct `Proven`/`Refuted`**. The present negative `Unreachable` verdict wrongly rests on its f64 infeasibility — this is the B1a hole of §1. The fix is not to make the LP exact but to demote it: keep it off the certificate path entirely.
- **(b) Exact rational (ℚ, fraction-free Bareiss) — required for rank, null-space, and the Farkas dual.** Rank is *discontinuous* in the matrix entries, so f64-plus-rounding is unsound: an arbitrarily small perturbation flips `rank = c − 1`. The cost is benign: ≈O(|P|²·|T|) integer operations with Hadamard-bounded intermediate entries (the Bareiss guarantee). This is the regime the P/T-semiflow null-space and the Farkas certificate live in — the workhorse of F1/F2.
- **(c) Exact integer (ℤ, Hermite/Smith normal form) — needed only where integrality or an exact-zero coefficient is the whole point.** The integer marking equation (does a *nonnegative integer* `σ` solve `m = m₀ + C·σ`?) is the canonical case. Deferred to a scoped B0b; it is not the default.

**Discriminating principle.** Exact integer SNF/HNF is required **iff** a certificate's validity depends on integrality or an exact-zero coefficient; otherwise the rational shadow, rechecked exactly, suffices. **SNF is the wrong default cost** — reaching for it everywhere pays integer-lattice prices for rational-shadow problems.

**Single separating invariant, not the generating set.** Minimal P/T-semiflow generators (Colom–Silva) are worst-case exponential. The *certificate* path must emit **one separating invariant** — a single polynomial null-space/LP solve — not the full minimal basis. The basis is needed only for *coverage* predicates (`is_covered_by_s_invariants` / `is_covered_by_t_invariants`), computed lazily and capped, off the fast path. This is backlog B1's certificate/coverage split, made concrete in F2 below.

**Cheapest keystone.** The cluster quotient (F4) is a near-linear union-find over the stored preset/postset slices — the highest leverage per line in the algebraic core. It unlocks both the Rank-Theorem count `c` and the S/T-component decomposition at once.

**The WSTS order-lift needs more than `TokenOps`.** Abstracting the engine over the order (F7) needs a `WellQuasiOrder` (the wqo obligation licenses termination) *plus* an `Ideal<D>` with a `join` (acceleration = the ideal join). The coordinatewise ω-promotion in the code is specialized to the product order and does not generalize without abstracting that join — `TokenOps` is genericity over the fiber, not over the order.

**The verdict.** B0 (the exact-rational core) is the load-bearing *soundness* precondition, not merely a capability. It is the only proposed move that closes an *active* soundness hole — the float Farkas dual plus the silent false-`Unreachable` of §1 — rather than adding reach.

### 4.2 · M4 build record — the exact-rational scalar (`Rational`, B0 scalar half)

> Records what M4 *built* — the scalar `S` the exact matrix layer (M5) stands on — and the *representation decision* the design (§4, §4.1) flagged as "the program's first engineering decision."

**The representation decision: `i128` with overflow *detection*.** `Rational` (`core/analysis/rational.rs`) is a normalized `i128 / i128`. Of the three candidates §4 named:
- `i64/i64` — **rejected**: silent wrap on the Bareiss intermediate determinants (Hadamard growth) is a soundness hole.
- **`i128` with detection — chosen.** Every operation is `checked_add`/`checked_sub`/`checked_mul`/`checked_div`/`checked_neg` returning `Result<Rational, RationalOverflow>`; an overflow is a *detected error*, **never a silent wrap**. There is deliberately **no `impl Add`/`Mul`** — an operator that can silently overflow would re-open the very hole the type closes — so a caller must thread the `Result` and an overflow forces an honest `Inconclusive` at the decision site. This is the integer analogue of "floating-point is a soundness concern" (doctrine #2): a wrong magnitude is a wrong certificate.
- `num-rational` (bignum) — **deferred** (a new dependency + per-op allocation), recorded as the promotion path *if a measured corpus need arises*; the `checked_*` signatures are exactly the seam an `i128 → bignum` swap slots into without changing call sites. The choice not to add the dependency now is the lean call (doctrine #9): `i128` carries the corpus's net sizes, and the failure mode if it does not is *loud*, not silent.

**Invariants and exactness.** Every `Rational` is normalized at construction (`gcd(|num|, den) == 1`, `den > 0`, canonical zero `0/1`), so `Eq`/`Hash`/`Ord` are **representation-independent** (`2/4` and `1/2` are the same bits). `i128::MIN` is refused by `new` (its magnitude is not representable — a detected failure beats an arithmetic panic), as is a zero denominator and division by zero. **Ordering is exact at every magnitude:** the fast path is `sign(a·d − c·b)`, and when that cross-product would overflow `i128`, comparison falls back to an **overflow-free continued-fraction (Euclidean) descent** — *no `f64` ever decides an ordering*, so the "exact" claim holds without exception (tested by `ordering_is_exact_even_when_the_cross_product_overflows`, which forces the fallback with `MAX/2` vs `MAX/3`).

**Gates (M4 `[PROP]`, discharged by finite-domain enumeration — the petrivet no-`proptest` convention).** Over a representative domain (`num ∈ −4..=4`, `den ∈ 1..=4`): field axioms — commutativity, associativity, distributivity, the additive identity/inverse (`a + (−a) == 0` exactly) and multiplicative identity/inverse (`a · a⁻¹ == 1`); value-equality independent of representation; `a − b == a + (−b)`; ordering exact and consistent with `sign(a − b)`. Policy gate: overflow is **detected, never wrapped** (`MAX + MAX`, `MAX · 2`, an overflowing denominator product all return `Err(RationalOverflow)`).

**Scope / runway.** M4 is the scalar half only. The matrix half (Bareiss `rank`/`kernel`/`left_kernel`/`farkas_certificate`), the float-`Unreachable` closure (B1a), and the exact prove-bounded P-subinvariant decider (A4) are **M5**, which is the first consumer of `Rational`; the `#![allow(dead_code)]` on the module (with its doctrine-#7 note) is removed when M5's matrix layer lands. SNF/HNF (the ℤ regime, §4.1 regime (c)) is **not** introduced — it is B0b, scoped to integer-marking refinement, the wrong default cost.

### 4.4 · Foundation brickwork — the soundness-hole remediation (post-M5 adversarial review)

> Records a remediation pass over the foundation that an **independent adversarial review** surfaced after M2/M5: each finding was reproduced as a running counterexample against the library's own state-space oracle (`is_reachable`/`is_live`/`is_coverable`/`is_bounded`), then fixed, then pinned by a regression test. The discernment behind each fix — *why* the hole was a hole, and why the chosen repair is sound from primitives — is recorded here so a successor can re-derive it (doctrine #8). The fixes restore *capability soundly* where possible; where full capability is not soundly achievable from primitives, the certificate carries and re-verifies the missing witness, or the checker abstains. The trust-boundary contract (§5) is the load-bearing invariant throughout: each `check` re-establishes its property against the *original* net, sharing no code with the generator beyond primitive net access (with the C1 exception that siphon/trap and minimal-siphon enumeration count as core net access).

**BLOCKER 1 — `ParikhVectorCert::check` minted a false `Proven` on a class fence alone.** The marking equation `M₀ + C·σ = target` is only *necessary* for reachability; it is *sufficient* on **live** marked graphs (Thm 5.2.7) and **live-and-bounded** free-choice nets with the trap check (Thm 5.3.18). The old checker accepted any class-`{MarkedGraph, FreeChoice, Circuit}` witness whose endpoint equation balanced — but a *non-live* net of the same class admits a balanced σ to a genuinely unreachable target. *Counterexample (oracle-confirmed):* a bounded, strongly-connected but non-live marked graph (one unmarked circuit kills `t0`,`t1`; only the other circuit holds a token); `{pd:1} + C·(t0:1,t1:1) = {pc:1}` balances, yet `{pc:1}` is `Unreachable`. Re-deriving the missing precondition (marked-graph liveness = every circuit marked; FC liveness = Commoner–Hack) needs the *liveness-analysis machinery* (circuit enumeration; the CHC engine) — **not** primitive access. **Fix:** accept iff σ **realizes** as an actual firing sequence — greedily fire any still-needed enabled transition (via `try_fire`/`is_enabled`) until the counts exhaust, then compare the *reached* `u32` marking to the target. A realized σ *is* a firing sequence, so it certifies reachability on **any** class with nothing left to trust; it shares no code with the generator, touches no `f64` and no unchecked `i64` (this also closes the **unsafe overflow direction**: the old `displacement_on` accumulated in plain `i64` that wraps in release — a wrapped sum coinciding with the target minted a false `Proven`; realization compares actual markings and has no such accumulator). Capability is *preserved and soundly extended*: greedy maximal realization is complete for the persistent classes the certificate targets (marked graphs; cluster-persistent free-choice), and a genuinely-realizable witness now validates even on a `General` net. Where realization gets stuck, the checker abstains — never a false positive. Pinned by `parikh_rejects_nonlive_marked_graph_unreachable_target`, `parikh_rejects_an_unrealizable_balanced_witness`, and `parikh_accepts_a_realizable_witness_on_a_general_net`.

**BLOCKER 2 — `SiphonTrapCoverCert::check` validated only the exhibited cover, never the universal.** Commoner–Hack is a *universal*: **every** (minimal) siphon must contain a marked trap. The old checker validated each exhibited `(siphon, marked-trap)` pair but never confirmed the cover was *complete*, so an **incomplete** cover that omitted the one offending unmarked-trap siphon passed — minting a false `Proven` liveness/deadlock-freedom on a net that is not live. *Counterexample (oracle-confirmed):* a non-live net carrying one genuinely marked siphon-trap pair `{p0,p1}` whose cover omits the unmarked offender siphon `{q0,q1}`; `is_live == false`, yet the partial cover passed. **Fix:** `check` re-establishes the universal *independently from primitives* — it re-enumerates the net's minimal siphons (`minimal_siphons`) and, for each, computes the maximal trap inside it (`maximal_trap_in`) and confirms it is non-empty and marked under `M₀`. Minimal-siphon enumeration and maximal-trap-in-a-set are *core net access* under the C1 exception (siphon/trap structure, not the liveness *decision* the generator makes); the checker assembles them into the universal itself, sharing no decision code with the generator's `commoner_hack_criterion`. The exhibited cover is still validated for internal consistency, but the verdict rests on the re-derived universal. The cost is the (worst-case exponential) re-enumeration — the honest price of re-establishing a *universal* from primitives, and the same structural object the generator computed. Pinned by `siphon_trap_cover_rejects_an_incomplete_cover` and `siphon_trap_cover_accepts_when_the_universal_holds`.

**BLOCKER 2 (second hole) — the same cover minted false `Proven` *liveness* on a non-free-choice net.** The first BLOCKER 2 cut re-established the universal but left the cert **polarity-ambiguous**: the ledger had one `SiphonTrapCoverCert` backing *both* free-choice liveness (`is_efficiently_live (FreeChoice via CHC)`) and general deadlock-freedom (`is_efficiently_deadlock_free`), and `check` returned `true` whenever the universal held — with no notion of *which* verdict it certified. But the Commoner–Hack universal is sufficient for **liveness only on free-choice nets**; on a general net it proves **deadlock-freedom only** (Murata Thm 12 / Primer Thm 5.17 — the repo's own `commoner_hack_criterion` doc records exactly this). So a cert whose universal holds, attached to a *liveness* candidate on a non-free-choice net, would mint a false `Proven` liveness — the cardinal sin, and the precise polarity-conflation the binding design flagged. *Counterexample (brute-force-found against the oracle, then minimized):* a `General` 4-place net (`t0:{p1,p3}→{p2}`, `t1:{p2,p3}→{p0,p1}`, `t2:{p2}→{p0,p1}`, `t3:{p0,p1}→{p1,p3}`, marked `{p0,p1,p3}`) that is **bounded**, **deadlock-free**, with **every minimal siphon marked-trap-covered** (so the universal genuinely holds), yet `is_live == false`. **Fix:** carry the verdict's meaning in the type — `SiphonTrapClaim { Live, DeadlockFree }` on the cert — and have `check` apply the class fence after the universal: a `Live` claim additionally requires `NetClass::is_free_choice` (a structural pre/post-cardinality tag, recomputable from primitives — *not* liveness machinery), abstaining otherwise; a `DeadlockFree` claim is accepted on any class. This makes the dangerous conflation **unrepresentable** (doctrine #4): a CHC pass on a general net can no longer be read as liveness. Capability is preserved on the cases the theorem covers (free-choice liveness still validates; deadlock-freedom on any class still validates). Pinned by `siphon_trap_cover_rejects_liveness_on_a_non_free_choice_net` (the `Live` claim is rejected while the `DeadlockFree` claim is accepted on the very same counterexample) and `siphon_trap_cover_accepts_deadlock_freedom_on_a_free_choice_net`. **Cost-relocation note (frontier-map correction):** the exact CHC re-derivation is exponential-in-|P| worst case and now sits inside the trusted-base accept path — there is no compact local certificate that a place-set list is the complete minimal-siphon set, so the universal must be *recomputed*, not re-validated; the C7 free-choice-liveness cell is "exponential-but-exact," not "polynomial in the exhibited cover." A budget-guard (return `false`/Inconclusive when over budget) is the deferred D1 follow-on. The `claim` field already makes *this* checker refuse the wrong-class liveness reading internally (the hole is closed at the C1 layer regardless of `accept`'s gate); the cross-cutting **polarity-coherence gate on `accept`** (A6) is now implemented as well (§4.7), so a wrong-signed candidate is refused at the boundary too — defence in depth.

**MAJOR 3 — negative `Uncoverable` rested entirely on `f64` microlp.** Both `analyze_coverability` arms (the rational and ILP covering LP) returned `Uncoverable` on the `f64` LP *failing* — the same silent-false hole B1a closed for reachability, here uncaught by the firewall (no positive object on the negative path). **Fix:** demote the `f64` covering LP off the verdict path entirely and add an exact covering guard `covering_invariant_exact` (the covering analogue of the B1a guard): a sound *sufficient* certificate is a non-negative place sub-invariant `y` with `yᵀ·C ≤ 0` and `yᵀ·target > yᵀ·M₀` (the weighted token count cannot grow, so no reachable marking can cover the heavier target). It is computed exactly from the M5 `left_kernel` (P-semiflows over ℚ) — scanning the basis and its negation for a non-negative separating vector. On `NotCertified`/`Overflowed` the verdict escalates to the **exact, always-terminating Karp–Miller coverability graph**, never fabricating from `f64`. Scanning the basis ± is sound but not complete (a separating sub-invariant that is only a non-trivial non-negative combination escalates to the graph); full exact covering-LP duality is a B-epic follow-on — *soundness here does not depend on completeness*. The three orphaned `f64` covering-LP functions were removed (lean; no caller, not wired as a filter). Pinned by `m3_coverable_target_never_reported_uncoverable` and `m3_exact_guard_certifies_uncoverable_via_subinvariant`.

**MAJOR 4 — the reachability ILP negative arm still fabricated `Unreachable` from `f64`.** B1a (M5) gated only the rational arm; the ILP arm (`MarkingEquationNoIntegerSolution`) returned `Unreachable` purely on the `f64` branch-and-bound failing. The exact ℚ guard cannot confirm an *integer-only* infeasibility (a target rationally feasible but with no non-negative integer firing vector is the ℤ/Smith-normal-form regime, deferred to B0b), so the `f64` ILP failing is *not* a sound negative. **Fix:** a single exact guard (`marking_equation_exact`) now subsumes *both* former `f64` arms — `Infeasible` over ℚ ⇒ sound `Unreachable` (carrying the separating P-invariant); `Feasible`/`Overflowed` (incl. every integer-only and degenerate case) ⇒ escalate to the exact, terminating state-space explorer, never fabricate. (The `f64` rational LP is retained off the verdict path as a documented-but-unused inexact filter and `literature.rs` anchor.) Pinned by `m4_ilp_arm_does_not_fabricate_unreachable_on_a_reachable_target` and `m4_exact_guard_still_refutes_token_nonconserving_target`.

**MAJOR 5 — positive `is_bounded()` rested on the `f64` `find_positive_place_subvariant`; the exact `BoundednessSubinvariantCert` was dead code.** A near-boundary `yᵀ·C` that the float simplex rounds to 0 could mint a false `bounded`. **Fix:** route the structural (marking-independent) positive answer through the exact certificate (`PetriNet::structurally_bounded_exact`): the `f64` LP *suggests* `y`, which is rationalized (round each weight, try small scalings) and re-verified by `BoundednessSubinvariantCert` over ℚ against the original net (`y > 0`, `yᵀ·C ≤ 0` per column). A validated certificate is a sound proof; a failed validation falls back to the exact coverability graph (`analyze_boundedness`). The verdict no longer rests on the `f64` value itself — the GRAT pattern (untrusted suggester, exact checker, sound fallback). The A4 *completion* (routing the live free-choice `is_efficiently_bounded` decider through an emitted S-component-cover certificate, B3) remains M6; the per-place `is_place_bounded` `f64` path is out of scope for this pass (a distinct item). Pinned by `m5_structurally_bounded_exact_certifies_a_conservative_net` and `m5_structurally_bounded_exact_rejects_an_unbounded_net`.

**Minors.** (i) `Rational::from_int` bypassed `new`'s `i128::MIN` guard, leaving a value whose magnitude is unrepresentable; it now upholds the same guard *loudly* (a documented panic precondition — no in-tree caller passes `i128::MIN`; arbitrary callers use `new`, which returns a `Result`). (ii) `TokenConservationCert` summed token counts via `Marking::total_tokens` (a `u32` sum that wraps in release — the *safe* direction for a negative certificate, but a verdict path must not depend on that); the check now accumulates in `u64`. The public `Marking::total_tokens` u32 sum is left unchanged (widening it is a wider ripple; the verdict-path widening is the soundness-relevant fix). (iii) The **polarity-coherence gate** on `accept` (the A6 firewall item) is now **implemented** — see §4.7 — not deferred: `Certificate` gained a `polarity() -> Polarity` method (each witness declares `ProveYes`/`ProveNo`), and `accept` requires it to agree with the candidate's pole before promoting, collapsing a mismatch to `Inconclusive(NotApplicable)` rather than minting a wrong-signed verdict.

**Independence / trust-boundary posture (the load-bearing check).** Every fix is **primitive-only** or **self-contained-witness-re-verifying**: BLOCKER 1 (realization) and the token-sum widening use only `fire`/`is_enabled`/marking; BLOCKER 2 re-derives the universal from the C1-exception siphon/trap+minimal-siphon primitives and gates the *liveness* reading on `NetClass::is_free_choice` (a structural pre/post-cardinality tag, recomputable from primitives, *not* the net's liveness-analysis machinery — the only `class()` call on the trusted path is this fence, which is itself a primitive recomputation); MAJORS 3–5 use the M5 exact-arithmetic kernel (exact dot product / left-kernel / Farkas) and, on any uncertified case, the exact state-space oracle — never `f64` on a verdict path, never the net's liveness/boundedness *decision* machinery. No checker shares generator *decision* code; the only generator-adjacent calls are the granted primitives. Where a precondition could not be re-derived from primitives (marked-graph/FC liveness), the certificate was re-grounded on a property that *can* be (realization for reachability; the free-choice class fence for the liveness-vs-deadlock-freedom split), rather than trusting the class tag.

### 4.7 · A6 — the polarity-coherence gate on `accept` (post-remediation)

The firewall (§3, the M1 build record) made `accept` the sole constructor of `Proven`/`Refuted`, gating on `cert.check`. But it gated on the check's *pass/fail* only, trusting the candidate's *polarity* blindly: a passing check on a `Decided::refute(_)` paired with a *positive* certificate would have minted `Refuted` even though the witness establishes the positive pole. Not a live hole at M2 (no decider calls `accept` yet, and each C1 checker is internally pole-correct), but a latent boundary gap that M3/C4 — which wires deciders through `accept` — would have turned live: a decider pairing a passing checker with the wrong-signed candidate would mint a wrong-signed yet check-backed verdict. This is the deferred **A6** item.

**What landed.** A6 is now enforced *structurally at the boundary*:
- `Certificate` gains `fn polarity(&self) -> Polarity` (no default — every witness must declare its pole, so no silent default can mis-sign). A single witness establishes exactly one pole, so implementors return `ProveYes`/`ProveNo`, never `Exact` (that is a *decider* capability). The six checkers declare: `FiringSequenceCert`, `ParikhVectorCert` (realization), `SiphonTrapCoverCert` (both `Live` and `DeadlockFree` are positive properties), `BoundednessSubinvariantCert`, `PlaceBoundednessSubinvariantCert` → `ProveYes`; `TokenConservationCert` (unreachability) → `ProveNo`. These agree with the `ledger.rs` polarity column (the A6 latent-structure surfacing).
- `Decided::polarity()` maps a candidate to its pole (`Proven → ProveYes`, `Refuted → ProveNo`).
- `accept` computes `coherent = cert.polarity() == Exact || cert.polarity() == candidate.polarity()` and promotes only if `coherent && cert.check(...)`; a mismatch collapses to `Inconclusive(NotApplicable)` — the same honest abstention as a failed check, never the opposite verdict. The coherence test short-circuits *before* `check`, so a misused certificate's (possibly expensive) check is never run.

The M1 type firewall is untouched: `Proof`/`Refutation`'s private fields and `into_verdict` (the sole filler, reachable only from `accept`) are unchanged; the gate adds a *second* boundary condition (pole agreement) ahead of the existing check, never a new constructor.

**Proven (gates).** `[PROP]` `accept_is_check_gated_for_all_polarity_outcome_combinations` (the positive-cert × refute-candidate case now yields `Inconclusive`, not `Refuted` — the corrected assertion) and the new `accept_enforces_polarity_coherence` (both mismatch directions against *passing* `ProveYes` and `ProveNo` certificates: positive-cert+refute → `Inconclusive`, negative-cert+prove → `Inconclusive`; matched poles → the decided verdict). Green gate: lib **218/0** (was 217/0; +1 coherence test), `checker_invariants` 9/0, `firewall_probe` 2/0; build/clippy clean, no new warnings. (This supersedes minor (iii) of §4.4 and the deferral noted in the BLOCKER 2 record — the gate is no longer deferred to M3.)

### 4.5 · Foundation brickwork (second pass) — two residual verdict-path holes the first pass left

> A follow-on adversarial review of the *first* brickwork pass (§4.4) found two soundness holes the first pass left open, **both on the live public decision path** (not on the M2 checkers it hardened). The first-pass §4.4 correctly fixed the certificate checkers and the *general* f64 verdict arms, but two verdict paths slipped through. Each is reproduced as a running counterexample against the library's own oracle, fixed, and pinned by a `[REGRESS]` test that fails on the pre-fix code (verified by reverting the fix and observing the failure). The trust-boundary contract (§5) and the "no verdict path may rest on f64, and exact arithmetic must never silently wrap" rule (§4.1) are the load-bearing invariants throughout.

**RESIDUAL 1 — the live-marked-graph reachability arm still rested on the `f64` ILP (both directions).** MAJOR 4 (§4.4) gated the *general* negative arm of [`analyze_reachability`](../../petrivet/src/api/system/reachability.rs) through the exact `marking_equation_exact` guard — but the **live-marked-graph** arm (`if self.class().is_marked_graph() && self.is_live()`) short-circuits with its own `return` *before* that guard, and was untouched. It called [`find_marking_equation_integer_solution`](../../petrivet/src/core/analysis/semi_decision.rs) (an `f64` ILP via `good_lp::microlp`, rounding with `v.round() as u32`) and:
> - the POSITIVE proof `LiveMarkedGraphMarkingEquationIntegerSolution` carried the **rounded** `f64` firing-count vector — a rounded vector is not a proof (it can be off-by-one and not actually solve the integer equation; even a genuine integer solution is only *necessary* in general — the BLOCKER 1 issue, here on the decider); and
> - the NEGATIVE `MarkingEquationNoIntegerSolution → Unreachable` rested on the `f64` ILP merely *failing* — a spurious `f64` infeasibility at a degenerate vertex is a silent false `Unreachable` the firewall cannot catch (the exact B1a/M4 hole, in this arm).
>
> **Fix (mirrors the BLOCKER 1 binding decision, applied to the decider this time):** use the `f64` ILP only as a *suggestion*, then **realize** σ as an actual firing word against the original net (`PetriNet::live_marked_graph_realized_word` → `realize_firing_vector`, greedy enabled-order replay via `try_fire`, the decider-side twin of `ParikhVectorCert::realize`). A realized σ *is* a firing sequence, so the emitted proof is now `ReachabilityProof::FiringSequence` — genuine, replay-checkable, no `f64`, no rounding, nothing to trust; on a live (persistent) marked graph the greedy realization succeeds whenever the equation is realizable, so capability is preserved. If the ILP suggests nothing or realization does not reach the target, the arm **falls through** (no `return`) to the exact `marking_equation_exact` negative guard and then the exact state-space explorer — never fabricating. The dead `MarkingEquationNoIntegerSolution`/`LiveMarkedGraphMarkingEquationIntegerSolution` proof variants are kept as doctrine-#7 blueprint markers (no producer). Pinned by `live_marked_graph_arm_is_sound_in_both_directions` (asserts a reachable target yields a *replayable* `FiringSequence` and an unreachable target is never reported `Reachable`, both against the oracle; the test fails on the pre-fix arm — it produces the rounded-ILP proof type).

**RESIDUAL 2 — the token-sum widening (minor ii) was applied to the checker but not to the deciders.** §4.4's minor (ii) widened `TokenConservationCert`'s sum to `u64`, but explicitly left the public `Marking::total_tokens`/`IdxMarking::sum` `u32` sums unchanged — and **those `u32` sums are read directly on the live decider verdict paths**, where they silently wrap in release (no overflow-checks in any `Cargo.toml`):
> - [`is_efficiently_reachable`](../../petrivet/src/api/system/reachability.rs) (Circuit / live-StateMachine arms) decides reachability by `self.marking.sum() == target.total_tokens()`. A `u32` wrap making two genuinely-different totals compare **equal** mints a false `Some(true)` — **the cardinal sin, on the public verdict path.** (Counterexample: initial total `5e9` wraps to `705_032_704`; target total `705_032_704` fits — wrapped sums coincide, true sums differ; the target is unreachable. Pinned by `token_sum_overflow_does_not_mint_false_reachable`.)
> - [`is_efficiently_live`](../../petrivet/src/api/system/liveness.rs) / `efficient_liveness` test `self.marking.sum() > 0` / `== 0`; a marked total that is a multiple of `2^32` wraps to exactly 0, minting a false `Some(false)`/all-`L0` (a live net reported dead). (Pinned by `marked_total_at_two_to_the_32_is_still_live`.)
> - [`efficient_boundedness`](../../petrivet/src/api/system/boundedness.rs) / `efficient_place_boundedness` set the bound to `self.marking.sum() as K`; a total of `2^32 + 1` wraps to bound `1`, so `is_safe`/`is_k_bounded(1)` mint a false safety verdict. (Pinned by `bound_magnitude_overflow_does_not_mint_false_safety`.)
> - the `analyze_reachability` S-net arm computed `iter().sum::<u32>()` for the token-conservation comparison.
>
> **Fix:** add non-wrapping `u64` accumulators — `IdxMarking::<u32>::wide_sum` (core) and `Marking::<u32>::total_tokens_wide` (api) — and route **every** verdict-deciding token-sum site through them (a `u32`-per-place marking totals at most `place_count · u32::MAX < 2^64`, so `u64` cannot itself overflow; bound magnitudes saturate at `usize::MAX` on a 32-bit target). The public `total_tokens`/`sum` stay `u32` (their callers are display/test, and changing the return type is a wider ripple) but now carry a doc-note that they are unsuitable for a verdict path. This is the integer analogue of "no verdict on `f64`": a silently-wrapped sum is a wrong magnitude and therefore a wrong verdict.

**Independence / trust posture.** Both fixes are **primitive-only**: RESIDUAL 1's realization uses only `fire`/`is_enabled`/marking against the original net (the `f64` ILP is a pure suggester, off the verdict path); RESIDUAL 2's widening is a pure arithmetic correction on the existing primitive token counts. No checker or decider gains any dependency on generator decision code; no new `f64` enters any verdict path.

**Flag for Michael.** (a) RESIDUAL 1 realization inherits the BLOCKER 1 persistence/completeness caveat (greedy realization is complete on the persistent classes the arm targets — marked graphs are persistent; a non-greedy-realizable σ would fall through to the state space, a *capability* not a soundness matter). (b) A robustness note, not a soundness hole: `realize_firing_vector` and the replay checkers call `try_fire`, whose `fire_unchecked` **panics** (not abstains) if a place would exceed `u32::MAX` during replay — an adversarial witness pumping >4e9 tokens into one place would panic the checker/decider rather than abstain. A panic cannot mint a false `Proven` (the safe failure mode), but the contract's spirit ("overflow ⇒ abstain") suggests a future `try_fire` variant that returns an overflow error the replay paths convert to abstention; deferred (changing `try_fire`'s panic contract is a wider ripple).

**Green gate (S4, second pass).** `cargo build -p petrivet` clean (only the 2 pre-existing `idx_arcs` warnings); `cargo clippy -p petrivet --all-targets` warning histogram **byte-identical to the pre-brickwork baseline** (verified by a worktree diff against commit `32eb583`); lib **199/0** (was 195/0; +4 regression tests — 3 overflow pins + 1 marked-graph-arm pin), `checker_invariants` 9/0, `firewall_probe` 2/0. ### F2 · Semiflows and invariants  — *Epic B1*

**Function.** The conservation laws of a net are the kernels of `C` and `Cᵀ`; the canonical generators are the minimal non-negative ones.

**Types.** `PInvariant` (a place weighting `y` with `yᵀC = 0`), `TInvariant` (`Cx = 0`), and the minimal semiflow generators (Colom–Silva / Martínez–Silva). These are the `Invariants` type and `compute_invariants` function that [`literature.rs`](../../petrivet/src/literature.rs) references but that do not exist.

**Algebra and laws.** The work splits along a sharp tractability seam (see the certificate/coverage split, §4.1):
- **The certificate (polynomial, fast path).** A *single separating* invariant attached to a negative verdict. The emitted invariant must be exact-rational and pass `y·C = 0 ∧ y·(m'−m₀) ≠ 0` in exact arithmetic *before the verdict returns*. One dot product checks it; it stays on the fast path.
- **The coverage (worst-case exponential, off the fast path).** `is_covered_by_s_invariants` / `is_covered_by_t_invariants` via minimal-semiflow generators is worst-case exponential; it is computed lazily and capped. S-invariant coverage implies conservative and bounded; T-invariant coverage implies consistent; each is verifiable in O(arcs) by one exact multiplication.

**Enables.** Conservativeness and consistency as certificates; structural boundedness as a checkable P-subinvariant rather than an `f64` vector (the polynomial *prove-bounded* certificate on general nets — backlog A4 — emitting `PositivePlaceSubvariant(y)` with the derived per-place bound `⌊(y·M₀)/y[p]⌋` and falling back to the coverability graph only when the LP does not decide); the soundness witness for implicit-place removal (Epic F); and the interface-correction term in Φ_inv (§8).

## 5. Validation layer

### F3 · Certificate and verdict calculus, checked against the original net  — *Epic A1 + Epic C1*

**Function.** A verdict is a checkable witness rather than a boolean; "undecided" is a distinct value, not an error; and trust is confined to the checkers — each of which re-establishes the property *against the original net*.

**Types.**
- `Verdict<P, N> = Proven(P) | Refuted(N) | Inconclusive`: three-valued, so that `Inconclusive` is type-distinct from `Refuted`. This type removes the L0 defect at [`liveness.rs`](../../petrivet/src/api/system/liveness.rs), where an unbounded net's transitions and a genuinely dead transition both receive `L0` — "unknown" and "provably non-live" become distinguishable.
- `trait Certificate { fn check(&self, net, m0, query) -> bool; }`. **The `query` argument is required**: a firing-sequence witness is meaningless without the target it claims to reach; a query-free property passes a unit `Query`. A firing sequence is checked by replay against `fire`/`is_enabled`; a Parikh vector by recomputing `m₀ + C·σ`; a P-invariant by one exact dot product (F1); a siphon/trap pair by re-evaluating its closure conditions. Certificate payloads are **owned and serializable** — they are the training label and the external-checker input (C6).
- `fn accept<C: Certificate>(c, net, m0, query) -> Verdict`: the only public constructor of `Proven`/`Refuted`. This function is the TCB in code.
- The `crate::model` module referenced by `literature.rs` (broken at line 409), which is the location of the per-property certificate types and the `Verdict`. Consolidating it resolves the five heterogeneous result shapes across `api/system/*` and unblocks `petrivet-wasm` (A3).

**The original-net checking invariant (the single most load-bearing decision).** Each checker must re-establish the property against the **original** `(net, m0, query)`, assuming nothing about which decider or reduction produced the witness, and sharing no code with the generators beyond primitive net access (`fire`, `is_enabled`, exact dot product). This is the line that:

- keeps the **trusted base constant under reduction-lifting** — a certificate produced on a reduced residual is `lift`ed and re-checked against the *original* net by the unchanged checker, so a buggy reduction or a buggy `lift` costs time, never correctness (Epic F);
- makes the format **tool-agnostic** — a certificate from a *different* generator (or, in principle, another tool) for the same `(net, query, verdict)` validates identically;
- makes `f` and the trusted-base size **meaningful** — the checkers are the only code whose correctness is load-bearing, and they are few, small, and replay-only (no solver, no graph machinery on the dot-product/replay paths).

**Algebra and laws.** Soundness law: `check(net, m0, query) == true ⇒ the claimed verdict holds for (net, m0, query)`; this is the only property trust depends on. Policy independence: the value of an accepted verdict is invariant under which decider produced the candidate and in what order — the soundness theorem stated as a structural property rather than an empirical one, and (per the inversion) the *enabling* property, not the headline.

**Enables.** The two `Some(false)` stubs — the marked-graph liveness arm in `liveness.rs` and [`is_covered_by_s_components`](../../petrivet/src/api/net/mod.rs) line 270, which returns a hardcoded `false` consumed for live FC nets at `boundedness.rs` line 67 — must either carry a witness or return `Inconclusive`/`None`; the type does not admit an unwitnessed positive verdict. Offline auditability of every result. Safety of the learned selection layer: a learned scheduler can change only which proofs are attempted, never whether an invalid one is accepted.

### F3′ · The certificate format and the checkable frontier  — *Epic C6/C7*

Two deliverables ride on F3 and are specified here because they are part of the trust architecture, not the algebra.

**An interoperable certificate format (C6).** Petri-net model checking lacks the DRAT/LRAT/GRAT/VeriPB analogue the SAT/ILP world has. Petrivet's proof objects are already owned, serializable, and borrow-free — the format is one `serde` derive and one net-anchoring convention away. Anchor certificates to PNML place/transition **names** (not internal indices): `Cert = (net_id, query, polarity, witness, theorem_id)`. The F3 checkers consume it; a hand-authored certificate for a verdict produced by a different procedure checks identically against the original net. Cross-tool adoption is recorded as a position, not a built claim.

**The map of the checkable frontier (C7).** Certificate strength is sharply non-uniform, and mapping it is a thesis deliverable (the per-property × polarity table of §6):

- *Near-linear-checkable:* positive reachability/coverability (firing words); LP-refuted unreachability/uncoverability (Farkas P-semiflows — one dot product); structural boundedness (place invariants); unboundedness (Karp–Miller self-covering lassos); k-safety; deadlock existence.
- *Polynomial:* free-choice liveness (an *exhibited* siphon/trap cover — checking the cover is linear even though enumerating all siphons is hard).
- *The wall:* general (non-free-choice) liveness has no known compact checkable certificate; *integer-only* infeasibility has no single Farkas dual (its honest witness is a cutting-plane / VeriPB-shaped derivation, worst-case super-polynomial).

Two emitted witnesses are currently incomplete and must be enriched before they check: the coverability ω-witness lacks the **pumping cycle** (lasso) the checker needs, and `LivenessMethod::MarkedGraph{}` carries no **circuit-token** data. A checkable liveness certificate for one class strictly beyond free-choice is the standout open, high-novelty target.

**Repair record — the firewall was a grep, not a type (adversarial gate, 2026-06-20).** The first M1 cut left `pub enum Verdict<P, N> { Proven(P), Refuted(N), Inconclusive(Abstention) }` with **public** decided variants. A public enum variant *is* a public constructor: any downstream crate could write `petrivet::model::Verdict::Proven(payload)` directly — no certificate, no `accept`. The implementer's `[PROP]` evidence ("constructed only in `accept.rs`, verified by grep") proved current in-crate *usage*, not *unforgeability*; grep cannot see the name-constructor the public variant *is*. This is exactly the "runtime hope" the §3 type invariant is supposed to replace, and it falsified the M1 `[PROP]` gate ("no public path yields `Proven`/`Refuted` without a passing `check`") at the type level — the single most load-bearing invariant of the thesis (doctrine #3/#4). A second, latent leak compounded it: `Verdict` derived `Deserialize` (on by default through `pnml → serde`), and the derive macro synthesises a hidden constructor of `Proven`/`Refuted` from arbitrary input (`serde_json::from_str::<Verdict<_,_>>("{\"Proven\":…}")`), an unchecked verdict trusted on its face.

The fix makes the decided variants **unforgeable**, carrying the firewall in the type rather than in a convention:

1. **Private-field payload wrappers.** `Proven`/`Refuted` now carry `Proof<P>`/`Refutation<N>` — newtype structs whose single field is `pub(super)` (visible only inside `crate::model`). The variants stay public for `match`/read, but `Verdict::Proven(_)` cannot be *name-constructed* from outside `model`, because no other module can fill (or even name-construct) a `Proof`. `into_verdict` (private, reachable only from `accept`) is the sole site that fills the field. `Inconclusive` stays freely constructible via the new `Verdict::inconclusive(reason)` — abstention is honest and bears no proof obligation. Read access is preserved: `proof()`/`refutation()` keep their `Option<&P>`/`Option<&N>` signatures (reaching through `Proof::witness`), and `Proof`/`Refutation` expose `witness`/`into_witness`.
2. **`Verdict` derives `Serialize` only, not `Deserialize`.** Emitting a verdict as JSON is harmless; reconstructing one from untrusted bytes is the firewall hole. The M1 `[LINT]` round-trip gate names *certificates*/contract types, and `Abstention` (no proof obligation) round-trips in full; the decided variants are write-only across serde until a C6/M2 deserialize-then-re-check path exists (deserialize a *certificate* payload, re-run `accept`).
3. **The falsifying gate now exists.** Three `compile_fail` doctests on `verdict.rs` assert that `Verdict::Proven(_)`, `Verdict::Refuted(_)`, and even `Verdict::Proven(Proof(_))` do **not** compile from outside the module — the executable "no public path" proof that was impossible to write against the public-variant design, and whose absence is why the hole shipped. `tests/firewall_probe.rs` is the positive complement (an external consumer can still build/read an abstention). The `firing_sequence_fails_against_a_different_query` `[PROP]` gate was strengthened from two hand-picked perturbations to a *generated family* (every place × a 16-element delta set), proving the universal "accept is `Inconclusive` whenever target ≠ reached," not two examples.

On the `[PROP]` discharge convention (the minor regress finding): petrivet's `[PROP]` gates are discharged by **exhaustive enumeration over a finite domain** where the domain is finite (`accept_is_check_gated_for_all_polarity_outcome_combinations` covers the whole 2×2 polarity×outcome space; the firing-sequence gate now quantifies over a generated perturbation family), and by representative cases where it is not — matching the M0 precedent. `proptest` is intentionally **not** a dependency at M1: the firewall's universal claim is discharged by `compile_fail` (a stronger, type-level quantifier than any value-level property test) and by finite-domain enumeration, so adding a generator crate would widen scope without strengthening the proof.

## 6. Routing and observation layer

### F4 · Quotient, sub-net, and composition  — *Epic B2 (quotient), B3 (decomposition)*

**Function.** The recurring operation is the quotient (collapsing a coupling into equivalence classes) and its inverse, the induced sub-net; composition is the adjoint operation.

**Types.** `Partition` over places ∪ transitions (union-find over the existing sorted preset/postset `UniqueSortedSlice`s) with the cluster equivalence (transitive closure of the flow relation), yielding the cluster count `c`. `SubNet`, the net induced by a unit or component, itself a `DenseNet`, so existing analyses apply recursively. `Interface`, the shared boundary places. `compose`, with a property-specific operator `⊗` (verdict join for booleans, direct sum plus an F2 interface correction for invariant spaces, maximum for place bounds).

**Algebra and laws.** Partitions form a refinement lattice; the cluster quotient is the finest one the Rank Theorem requires. The cheapest keystone in the algebraic core (§4.1): one union-find yields the partition near-linearly and unlocks *both* the Rank-Theorem count `c` (checked against `rank(C) = c − 1` once F1 lands) and S-/T-component extraction. The parsed-but-unused NUPN unit forest ([`nupn.rs`](../../petrivet/src/api/pnml/nupn.rs)) becomes a `Partition` source — and carries its own free `unit_safe` (one-token-per-unit) safety certificate (backlog B8), if the forest is preserved through conversion rather than flattened. Law for `⊗`: associative, with `compose(parts) = whole` exactly when the property factors over the cut.

**Enables.** The cluster count for the Rank Theorem (and the certified replacement for the `is_covered_by_s_components` stub via S-component decomposition with exact free-choice bounds); the S/T-component decompositions; certified reductions (Epic F); and the partition lattice that Φ_bound and Φ_inv minimize over (§8).

### F5 · Decider, policy, and driver  — *Epic D1; the registry precedes Epic B*

**Function.** Make the execution schedule a parameter. Represent each decider as a value with metadata, order them by a policy, gate every acceptance on a certificate. The registry is sequenced **before** the structural generators (Epic B): B's generators are most cleanly *born* as `Decider` impls (and `Reduction` witnesses), so the registry refactor should precede them.

**Types.** `trait Decider { fn polarity() -> Polarity; /* ProveYes | ProveNo | Exact */ fn cost_class() -> CostClass; fn admissible(NetClass) -> bool; fn run(net, query, budget) -> Outcome<Verdict>; }`; a `Policy` whose default reproduces the current hand-coded `match self.class()` cascade exactly; a `Driver` that selects admissible deciders by domain, orders them by the policy, gates on the certificate, and returns the first `Proven`/`Refuted` or else `Inconclusive`. Associated type: `Budget`/`Cancellation`, a cooperative cancellation token threaded into the exploration loop and the solver calls — absent today (the only early exit is the ω short-circuit and process-level `catch_unwind`), and a prerequisite for both the adaptive policy (Epic D-ladder) and bounded per-decider measurement.

Polarity is not new metadata invented here: it is latent in the split proof enums and the prove-NO-only LP filters, and the registry surfaces it (backlog A6). It is what lets the two new deciders below declare themselves cleanly.

**Two new deciders the registry admits (backlog B10, B11).**
- **Continuous (fluid) relaxation — a class-agnostic `ProveNo` decider (B10).** The continuous relaxation (markings in ℝ≥0, fractional firing) is a sound over-approximation of discrete reachability/coverability, and continuous reachability/coverability/boundedness are **PTIME** (Fraca–Haddad 2015). It is the natural apex of the LP→ILP cascade already in `semi_decision.rs`, strictly tighter than the state-equation LP, and uniquely **class-agnostic**: it can decide *general, unbounded* instances at the ω-frontier where `reachability.rs` returns `Inconclusive` today. Witness: the Farkas/place-invariant `y` (F2) for the algebraic refutation, or the maximal firing set plus blocking empty siphon for the firing-set refutation. Checker (original-net): a dot product, resp. a polynomial firing-set fixpoint recompute.
- **General-net deadlock-*free* siphon certificate (B11).** The CHC engine ([`siphon_trap.rs`](../../petrivet/src/core/analysis/siphon_trap.rs) line 370) is, for *general* nets, a sound *sufficient* condition for deadlock-freedom (the `Ok` arm: every minimal siphon contains a marked trap). The dispatch discards this certifying value outside free-choice. Exposing it yields a certifying deadlock-free verdict for general nets; the checker confirms each exhibited place set is a siphon containing a marked trap — linear per pair, the generator bearing the enumeration cost. (The *converse* — an unmarked siphon as a reachable-deadlock witness — is **not** sound in general and is explicitly excluded.)

**Enables.** The cascade as data; the learned selection layer; parallel racing of deciders; anytime results.

### F6 · Measurement and cost (downstream only)  — *Epic D2/D3; G the rig*

**Function.** Execution cost has no canonical origin (machine speed is an additive shift in log-cost); only differences are comparable across machines. The layer observes and does not act.

**Types.** `Cost` in an affine space (raw fibers tagged with run context; derived differential invariants — rankings and log-ratios). `Observation` and the differential `FitnessComparison`. `Features` φ(N), assembled from the cached accessors (`NetClass`, strong connectivity, counts) plus the F2 invariant dimensions, F4 component and cluster counts, and the NUPN unit-tree shape. A separate crate (`petrivet-observe`) whose dependency arrow points only at the core (CI-enforced via `cargo tree`). Specified in the [self-measurement harness plan](../self-measurement-harness-plan.md).

**Enables.** The self-labeling training set; machine-portable regression tests; an always-on **soundness sentinel** that detects trusted-but-incorrect deciders (every `Decided` row with a known oracle must agree — the live regression for the §3 stub fixes). This sentinel, the corpus driver, and the differential no-regression test are the substrate the thesis rig (Epic G) promotes to thesis grade, and the source of the headline `f_struct`.

## 7. Transversal component

### F7 · Order abstraction (second phase)  — *Epic B9; far horizon H1*

**Function.** The engine is generic over the fiber (`TokenOps`) but not over the order; the order is the one un-abstracted element (the hand-rolled `impl<T:Ord> PartialOrd for IdxMarking<T>` and the `merge_ordering` fold in [`marking.rs`](../../petrivet/src/core/marking.rs)). `Omega` is the ideal completion of ℕ and the coordinatewise ω-promotion is the *ideal join*, neither captured by `TokenOps` (see §4.1). F7 abstracts it.

**Types.** `trait WellQuasiOrder` (with the test-enforced wqo obligation that licenses termination); a generalization of `Omega` to `Ideal<D>` (the ideal completion of an arbitrary WQO, with a `join`-based acceleration; `Omega` is the ℕ instance). ω-acceleration is restated as "join with the limit of the dominating chain."

**Enables.** The *near-term* payoff is not the WSTS zoo (that is the far horizon, H1) but converting the blanket `Inconclusive` at the ω-frontier into an Abdulla-style **backward-coverability refinement** carrying a partial over-approximation certificate (backlog E1; [`UniqueSortedSlice`](../../petrivet/src/core/unique_sorted_slice.rs) is the natural finite-basis representation). Because it lowers the `Inconclusive` rate it both widens coverage and increases training signal; it is a second phase, not future work. The full WSTS reuse — lossy channel systems, ν-nets, broadcast protocols, BVASS — is recorded as a horizon.

## 8. Dependent component

### F8 · Compositional analysis — the two factorization residuals  — *Epic H (dissolved to H2a/H2b)*

The scalar `Φ_PN = min over the partition lattice of distance(whole verdict, ⊗ of part verdicts)` is **dissolved** (§0). The single net-level scalar, the boolean-verdict version (assume-guarantee reasoning, a mature field), and the necessity claim do not survive. What remains are two *per-property* residuals, each computable, monotone, theorem-backed-zero, and non-vacuous; the **deliverable is their measurement over the corpus**, indexed by `NetClass`, not the metaphysics.

- **Φ_bound — the boundedness-factorization residual (schedule first; backlog H2a).** `Φ_bound = min over cuts (NUPN units / S-component cover) of Σ_p (b⊗(p) − b(N)_p)`, where `b⊗(p)` is the in-block structural bound with interface transitions made free. Monotone (`b⊗ ≥ b`), so well-signed; **provably 0** on live bounded free-choice nets (Hack) and strongly-connected T-nets (the circuit theorem); **> 0** (indeed ∞) on nets bounded only through cross-block synchronization. The minimizing cut is an F3-checkable witness.
- **Φ_inv — the invariant rank-defect residual (the novel Rank-Theorem link; backlog H2b).** `Φ_inv = min over cuts of dim ker(Cᵀ) − dim(⊕ block-local invariants, interface-corrected)` — the count of conservation laws no single block can see. Integer, basis-free, non-negative (with the explicit interface correction zeroing interface-coupled coordinates). Its link to the Rank Theorem (`rank C = c − 1`, hence to the cluster count `c` of F4) is the genuinely novel object, pending a full literature check against Kronecker / compositional methods.

Scope constraint: these are the factorization-residual quantities only; no stochastic or integrated-information-theory apparatus is introduced. The qualitative analyzer lacks the stochastic semantics that apparatus requires, and IIT is absent from the repository.

## 10. Sequencing and cross-cutting decisions
