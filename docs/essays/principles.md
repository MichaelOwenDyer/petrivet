# Principles of petrivet

> Status: technical overview. This document distills the library's architecture into four principles and the result they converge on. It distinguishes throughout between what the code currently implements (verifiable line by line) and where the analysis is proposed rather than implemented. Companion to the other essays in this folder; the convergence the four principles point to is stated in [README.md](../../README.md) and developed in the dedicated essays.

Every type, algorithm, and outstanding `// todo` in the library descends from four principles. Each principle is stated below, then developed downward through its intermediate ideas to the concrete details anchored in the code. Principles 1–3, together with the proof-carrying, decidability, and claim-honesty aspects of Principle 4, describe the implemented architecture and can be checked against the source directly. The remainder — the generalizations, the certificate calculus, the learned portfolio — is proposed rather than implemented, and is marked as such throughout.

The four principles converge not on a single net-level scalar but on a program: a **certificate** any third party can re-check (a small external checker as the entire trusted base), a measured **coverage** fraction that says how far the cheap structural tier reaches on the real corpus, and a soundness **firewall** that keeps both safe under any scheduling policy. That convergence is stated once in [README.md](../../README.md) and developed in the dedicated essays — [the checkable frontier](the-checkable-frontier.md), [the coverage claim](the-coverage-claim.md), [soundness as a free variable](soundness-as-a-free-variable.md), and [the factorization residual](the-factorization-residual.md). This essay derives the four principles; it does not re-derive their convergence.

---

## Principle 1: The substrate

*A transition affects only its preset and its postset; the net is a single matrix. The remaining theory is read off that arithmetic, and the global hardness of the net is not localized in any single transition.*

This is the most concrete object in the system and the parent of every structure built above it. A marking is a vector in ℕ^P; firing a transition subtracts its preset and adds its postset; and the net's entire linear theory is a single matrix, the incidence matrix **C = Post − Pre**. Locality is explicit in the source, and locality is precisely why the *global* question is hard.

### The microphysics is a free commutative monoid of purely local updates

`fire` and `is_enabled` consume one transition's pre- and post-set and reference nothing else; there is no global state, no shared clock, and no nonlocal interaction. This locality is not only a modeling convenience: the markings under addition form the free commutative monoid on the places (the sense in which "Petri nets are monoids"), which is what makes composition itself an algebraic operation later. It also allows the data structures to be cache-friendly: a transition's neighbours form a small local set, stored contiguously, so the hot loops touch contiguous memory. It is also why a net with no places or no transitions is rejected at build time as degenerate — a substrate with nothing to update has no associated dynamics. The structure-first dispatch that dominates Principle 3 is a consequence of this locality: because each transition is local and typed by the net's shape, the shape can be read before any computation.

### The incidence matrix is the shared spine

The incidence matrix is the single object every algorithm reads. The state equation **m = m₀ + C·σ** — where σ is the Parikh firing-count vector — is a *necessary* condition for reachability: if no non-negative σ solves it, the target is unreachable; if one exists, reachability is only *possible*, because the equation is blind to firing order. This necessary-but-not-sufficient asymmetry is not a weakness; it is the seam the dispatch exploits, because the "no" answer is cheap and certain while the "yes" answer requires further work. In the code the algebraic step is phrased as a feasibility query handed to a linear-programming solver rather than computed directly; the library does not perform the linear algebra so much as delegate it. The matrix itself is deliberately minimal: `IncidenceMatrix` is essentially `new` and `get`, a static object with no rank, no elimination, and no null-space computation. The richer algebra it could carry is named in `literature.rs` and not yet implemented — the thread Principle 2 develops.

### Local rules, intractable whole

The gap between the cheap local law and the expensive global question is genuine, not an artifact of implementation. General-net reachability is Ackermann-complete; boundedness and coverability are EXPSPACE-hard. No amount of engineering removes this — the difficulty is an emergent property of composing local rules, and it is permanent. Everything above this principle is, in one way or another, a means of *avoiding* the worst case on the instances that do not require it.

## Principle 2: The completion

*Rather than enumerate the state space, complete it. Adjoin the limit point the process repeatedly approaches, and the infinite fits inside a finite type. One engine runs over ℕ or over its completion; the boundary between the two is a single line of code.*

Completion is the operation of adding ideal limit-points to a space so that a process which only ever *approaches* an answer now *reaches* one. petrivet performs it twice — once on the order, once on the algebra — and both completions over-approximate reachability without enumerating it.

### One state-space engine, parameterized by a single scalar trait

The reachability graph and the coverability graph are not two algorithms; they are one generic engine parameterized by `TokenOps`, the trait abstracting the per-place token scalar — its zero, its successor, its "at least one" predicate. There are exactly two implementors, `u32` and `Omega`, and the genericity is over the *value living in each place* (the fibre), not over the order on states. Running the engine over `u32` yields reachability; running it over `Omega` yields Karp–Miller coverability. The two are made identical by a single function: a bounded coverability graph — one with no ω anywhere — *is* a reachability graph, recovered by unwrapping each `Finite(k)` to `k`. One engine; the token type determines which case applies.

#### Omega as the ideal completion of ℕ

The `enum Omega { Finite(u32), Unbounded }` represents infinity as a type. Its arithmetic is absorptive — incrementing ω is a no-op, ω plus anything is ω, and ω dominates every finite value — which is exactly the structure of the ideal completion of (ℕ, ≤): the principal ideals ↓k together with the top ideal ℕ itself. So when the engine compares a concrete `u32` marking against an `Omega` marking, it is comparing a *state* against an *ideal* — the Finkel–Goubault-Larrecq theory of ideal completions of well-quasi-orders, specialized to ℕ. The ω-acceleration is the operative step: it walks the ancestors of a newly discovered marking and, wherever the new marking strictly dominates an ancestor, promotes the strictly-greater coordinates to ω. Boundedness then reduces to the simplest predicate — the *absence* of the infinity symbol anywhere in the graph — and the coverability graph, as an over-approximation, refutes coverability with certainty while only approximating the exact "yes." This is why a separate short-circuit consults the cheaper coverability route before committing to the full reachability construction.

This identification is *real*, and it earns a name in the planning: because `Omega` literally is the ideal completion of ℕ, the existing engine is a latent well-structured-transition-system. The order is the one component left un-abstracted, below.

#### The order is the one un-abstracted component, and Dickson's Lemma is why it terminates

Everything in the engine is abstracted except the order itself: the componentwise product order on markings, a blanket `impl<T: Ord> PartialOrd for IdxMarking<T>` whose `merge_ordering` fold returns `None` the moment two coordinates disagree in direction. That order being a well-quasi-order — Dickson's Lemma, that (ℕ^P, ≤) has no infinite antichain and no infinite strictly-descending chain — is precisely what guarantees ω-acceleration terminates.

*This is where the design is proposed rather than implemented:* the order is the natural generalization seam. A repository-wide search for `WellQuasiOrder`, `WSTS`, `Ideal`, or `Dickson` finds nothing; the genericity stops at the fibre. The near-term payoff of lifting the order into a `WellQuasiOrder` trait (carrying the test-enforced wqo obligation that licenses termination) is *not* the WSTS zoo — that is a far horizon — but converting the blanket `Inconclusive` at the unbounded frontier into an Abdulla-style backward-coverability refinement that carries a partial over-approximation certificate. Lifting it further would let the same `DenseStateGraphExplorer` drive lossy channel systems, data nets, broadcast protocols, and the wider WSTS class — recorded, not scheduled.

Two nearer extensions build on this same order. Liveness is read off the strongly-connected-component condensation of the reachability graph — itself a quotient — with the L0–L4 ladder distinguishing dead, quasi-live, and fully live transitions (L4 exactly when the transition labels an edge in every *terminal* SCC); liveness is a graded property, not a boolean flag. And at the unbounded frontier, where the library currently returns `Inconclusive` the moment ω appears, the proposed next completion is a backward coverability analysis over upward-closed sets — for which `UniqueSortedSlice`, with its O(n+m) subset and intersection scans, is already the natural representation of a finite basis, and the ω-marking the analysis already returns is the seed an over-approximation refinement loop would tighten.

### The algebra completes the matrix into its kernel; the Farkas dual is the certificate of that completion

If the order semantics completes reachability *upward* into ideals, the algebraic semantics completes it into *conservation laws*. The P-invariants are the kernel of Cᵀ — the place-weightings y with yᵀC = 0, each one bounding a weighted token-sum for all time; the T-invariants are the kernel of C, the firing-count vectors that return the net to its initial marking. Both semantics are completions; both avoid enumeration; they are the dynamic and structural faces of the same approach, and classical Petri-net theory has always been the dialogue between them.

#### When the solver proves "no," it computes a conservation law that explains the impossibility — and discards it

When the marking-equation LP is *infeasible*, Farkas's lemma guarantees a dual certificate: a place-weighting y that is an actual P-invariant witnessing the contradiction — yᵀC = 0 with y·(m′ − m₀) ≠ 0 — the precise conservation law the target violates. The solver computes it. The code returns a payload-free `MarkingEquationNoRationalSolution` and discards it.

*This is a proposed extension, and it carries a soundness caveat the certificate program must take seriously.* Extracting that dual would make the negative verdict carry a certificate symmetric to the positive ones — checkable by a single dot product, human-legible as a conservation law. But there is a sharper point: today's `Unreachable` verdict rests on a *floating-point* solver *failing* to find a rational solution, and a spurious floating "infeasible" on a genuinely feasible rational system yields a silent false `Unreachable` — worse than the better-known stubs because it is data-dependent and invisible, and the firewall does not protect it (there is no positive object to check on the negative path). So the discipline is exact: the emitted invariant must pass yᵀC = 0 ∧ y·(m′−m₀) ≠ 0 in *exact* arithmetic before the verdict returns, which means an exact-rational matrix core, not a rounded `f64` dual. (In the reduction calculus discussed under Principle 4, that same exact dual becomes the soundness witness that licenses removing an implicit place.)

#### Every structural good-behavior property is itself a conservation-law certificate

The vocabulary is already dual and already present in `literature.rs`: full conservativeness is exactly coverage by positive S-invariants; consistency is coverage by positive T-invariants; structural boundedness is the existence of some y > 0 with yᵀC ≤ 0 (the weaker, super-invariant relative of conservation, and the condition the structural-boundedness LP actually tests). In the marked-graph case the token count on each circuit is itself an invariant — closed under firing — so an unmarked circuit is dead permanently and a marked one supports liveness, a fact Principle 3 converts into an exact decision.

### The completion is the named-but-unimplemented half; building it is completion, not invention

The order completion is implemented; the algebra completion is currently a documented absence. P- and T-invariants are documented at length and computed nowhere — `compute_invariants` and the `Invariants` type are doc-links resolving to nothing. The enabling component is small: an exact-rational matrix core over the existing dense `IncidenceMatrix`. That core provides *rank* — the missing ingredient for the Rank Theorem of Principle 3 — and null-space bases, which are the P- and T-semiflows the literature module documents; and with them the marking equation becomes an Esparza–Melzer-style refinement loop, re-solved with trap and siphon constraints whenever the firing-order-blind equation reports a spurious "maybe." Building the invariant layer gives the linear semantics the same certificate-bearing form the order semantics already has — and it is the generator behind several of the most valuable certificates: the exact separating S-invariant for unreachability, the structural boundedness bound, the residuals of the far horizon.

## Principle 3: The structural class

*Choose the algorithm by the net's shape before computing. Reduce a set by one bad-place rule to its fixpoint, and one closure answers a question about every subset at once. Where the net has the right structure, the over-approximations become exact.*

There is a region of the theory where the two over-approximations of Principle 2 stop being approximations and become exact, polynomial decisions. A closure is a monotone shrinking map iterated to its fixpoint; a quotient collapses a coupling into clusters; and within this region these become exact verdicts, gated by recognizing the net's class. Duality runs through the whole region as a reflection — siphon and trap, S- and T-invariant, S- and T-component, conservative and consistent. This is the philosophical center of the library: the README's promise to "choose the most efficient algorithm for your net's structure" is the promise to use these exact decisions whenever the net's class permits. It is also, operationally, the engine of the headline result: this tier is what the coverage number measures.

### Try structure first; fall back to search only when shape cannot decide

petrivet is in effect a portfolio solver that does not yet describe itself as one. For each property it runs an ascending-cost cascade of partial deciders, each gated by `self.class()`: for reachability, trivial equality, then S-net token conservation, then a live-T-net integer marking equation, then a rational LP filter, then an integer ILP filter, then full state-space exploration — each tried only if the cheaper ones were inconclusive. It is fast not because it solves the worst case but because it routes around it, using the structural decisions first and paying for search only when shape genuinely cannot decide. This is the operational meaning of search-versus-structure: structure is the escape from state-space explosion, and the cascade spends structure first.

The fraction of real corpus queries this tier decides *without exploration* is the headline number, `f_struct` — counted in queries decided, with two denominators (in-scope, and all-MCC counting out-of-scope as abstain), and validated family-held-out. A floor for it is measurable today, from the existing method tags and a single timing wrapper; the generators of Principles 2 and 3, as they land, raise it. (See [the coverage claim](the-coverage-claim.md).)

### On free-choice nets, liveness and boundedness become exact polynomial decisions

This is where the cheap reason *is* the correct answer, and it is worth cataloguing the exact-decidable classes, because each is a region the dispatch can decide with certainty. For **S-nets** (state machines): live iff strongly connected and marked; safe iff at most one token; reachability is exact under the rational solve, because total unimodularity makes the LP relaxation integral. For **T-nets** (marked graphs): live iff every directed circuit carries a token; each place's bound is the minimum token-count over the circuits through it; reachability is the integer marking equation plus a no-empty-circuit check. For **free-choice** nets: Commoner's theorem decides liveness in polynomial time — live iff every proper siphon contains a marked trap — and a live, bounded free-choice net decomposes into S-components covered by one-token invariants, from which its bounds follow; for **asymmetric-choice** nets the marked-trap condition weakens to *sufficient but not necessary*, and the code notes this. The coverage dualities tie it together: S-component coverage gives conservativeness, T-component coverage gives consistency. Each of these is a theorem where Principle 2's over-approximation collapses onto the exact answer because the net's shape excludes the pathologies the approximation was guarding against — and each yields a *polynomially checkable* certificate, which is what makes the structural tier part of the trusted result rather than a fast guess.

#### Siphon and trap are exact De Morgan duals; one closure settles an existential over every subset of places in a single computation

A siphon is a place-set that, once unmarked, can never regain a token; a trap is its dual — once marked, it never wholly empties. In the code `maximal_siphon_in` and `maximal_trap_in` are the *same* shrinking loop — "while some place is bad, remove it" — with presets and postsets swapped: exact De Morgan duals, each a closure operator that terminates because the set only ever shrinks, returning the unique maximal siphon (or trap) inside any given set. `commoner_hack_criterion` uses this efficiently. The Commoner criterion needs to determine whether *every* minimal siphon contains a marked trap — an existential quantifier over all subsets of each siphon. Rather than enumerate traps, it computes, for each minimal siphon, the maximal trap *inside* it, because "the siphon contains a marked trap" holds exactly when "the maximal trap in the siphon is marked." One closure operator settles a question about every subset in a single computation. The result is returned as a `Result` carrying, on success, every siphon paired with its marking witness, and on failure the exact unmarked siphon that can starve — a deadlock certificate. Positive evidence or a counterexample, never a bare bit.

That siphon and trap are dual closures is a *real* structural fact, and it has direct consequence for the checkable frontier: free-choice liveness has a *polynomial* certificate — an exhibited siphon/trap cover — because checking an exhibited cover is linear even though enumerating all siphons is hard. The cost lives in the generator, not the checker. (The honest scaling caveat: minimal-siphon enumeration is itself worst-case exponential and sits on the CHC path; it must be capped or scoped.)

### The missing construction is the cluster quotient — one partition that unlocks both halves

A recurring structural fact of the codebase is that every construction in it is an *order*, a *completion*, a *closure*, or a *quotient*: the marking order and the ideal completion (Principle 2); the siphon and trap closures (here); and the quotients still to come. This four-way reading is genuinely useful for organizing the code, and it is worth stating plainly that it is *exposition, not a theorem*: it names a pattern but predicts no new result, and of the four constructions only `WellQuasiOrder` and `Closure` earn a trait now (each with two implementors); a `Quotient` or `Completion` trait over a population of one would be aesthetics, not generality. With that fence in place, the missing construction here is the last of the four: the **cluster quotient**. A cluster is the equivalence class generated by "place s feeds transition t" — the connected components of the place-transition coupling — and with presets and postsets already stored as sorted slices, a union-find computes the partition cheaply. That single quotient unlocks two results at once. It yields the cluster count c that the Rank Theorem needs — and paired with the exact-rational rank routine from Principle 2, the theorem becomes a polynomial, certificate-bearing decision of simultaneous liveness-and-boundedness (well-formed iff rank C = c − 1), replacing the `is_covered_by_s_components` stub that today returns a hardcoded `false`. And it yields the S- and T-component decompositions the literature module already documents as if they existed. The word *cluster* appears exactly once in the crate — in a doc-comment transcribing the Rank Theorem — with no construction beneath it.

## Principle 4: The epistemic law

*Compute the reason, not just the answer — a finite witness a skeptic can recheck. Then allow any heuristic to choose what to try, because only the witness is believed. "Cannot yet decide" is an accurate result, not a failure.*

This is the principle that explains *why* the other three are run, and it is the principle the ratified direction places at the center. Principles 1–3 describe what petrivet builds; this one describes what a build is *for*. It has several aspects: the founding design question answered, the trust boundary made into the signature contribution, and the method underlying the whole construction.

### Refuse to enumerate — and the *reason* for the refusal is the decidability sweet spot

A basic place/transition net cannot test a place for emptiness — there is no zero-test — and that single limitation is *exactly* why its core questions remain decidable. Universality was traded for analyzability. The proof of the trade is one step away: adding an inhibitor arc, which is a zero-test, allows construction of a Minsky machine, regaining Turing-completeness and losing decidability at once. So every extension on the roadmap — weighted arcs, capacities, reset and transfer arcs, inhibitor arcs — is not a free feature but a deliberate purchase, each gain in expressive power paid for in a loss of what can be decided; the worst-case complexity is the price, and the operative question on real instances is a distinct object. The refusal to enumerate is deliberate: it keeps the analysis inside the fragment where reasons are cheaper than answers.

### A verdict is a witness, not a bit; the boolean is the residue — and the witness is the stone

The library's central commitment is that an analysis result should not be a boolean. The verdicts carry genuinely different witnesses: a scalar token-sum for the state-machine case, a Parikh firing-count vector for the marked-graph case, a replayable firing sequence for the general case, a firing sequence *and* an ω-marking for coverability, a siphon/trap pair or its counterexample for Commoner. The shape of each proof is dictated by the theorem that earned it; this is the vocabulary of *certifying algorithms*, where a procedure returns not just an answer but a witness a skeptic can check while trusting nothing about the procedure.

*This is the signature contribution of the project, and today it is a consistent practice rather than a formal calculus.* The five properties use five different result shapes; the `Result<evidence, counterexample>` pattern that fits appears in only one place; and the proofs are inert — there is no `verify()`, no `Certificate` trait, nothing that re-runs the cheap check. Yet the data is already certificate-shaped and already serializable: a firing sequence checks by replay against the existing `is_enabled`/`fire`; a marking-equation proof checks by recomputing m₀ + C·σ from its stored Parikh vector; a siphon/trap pair checks by re-verifying the closure conditions; the Farkas dual, once extracted exactly, checks by one dot product. The work the ratified direction puts first is to make this into an interoperable, machine-checkable object: a `Certificate::check(net, m0, query)` that re-establishes the property against the **original** net — the `query` argument required, because a firing-sequence witness is meaningless without the target it claims to reach — assuming nothing about which decider or reduction produced the witness and sharing no code with the generators beyond primitive net access. A name-anchored serialization (referencing PNML place/transition names, not internal indices) makes a certificate produced by one procedure check identically against another, in principle across tools. The payoff is that the trusted base shrinks to `{checkers} ∪ {remaining bare-boolean deciders}`, measured and required non-increasing; with the checkers small and (aspirationally) verified, trust is *concentrated* rather than diffuse.

This is what makes the certificate *the stone* rather than one nicety among several. The map of where compact checkable certificates exist — and where complexity theory forbids them — is itself a deliverable: positive reachability/coverability check by firing words; LP-refuted unreachability by one Farkas dot product; structural boundedness by place invariants; free-choice liveness by an exhibited siphon/trap cover (polynomial). And the wall: general (non-free-choice) liveness has no known compact checkable certificate, and *integer-only* infeasibility has no single Farkas dual — its honest witness is a cutting-plane / proof-logging-shaped derivation, worst-case super-polynomial. A checkable liveness certificate for one class strictly beyond free-choice is the standout open target. (See [the checkable frontier](the-checkable-frontier.md).)

### State only what can be shown: a claim is strongest when it states exactly what it is

The same discipline the verdicts apply to proofs, the project applies to its claims. A claim is strongest not when it is emphatic but when it states exactly what it is, and the gradation matters: what can be *seen* in the code, what is *inferred* from it, what is *imagined*, what is *believed*, and what is *promised*. Precision, not emphasis, removes the handholds for doubt. The discipline is visible in the verdicts themselves: Commoner is an *iff* on free-choice nets and merely *sufficient* off them, and the code marks the difference rather than overclaiming. The tool returns `Inconclusive` rather than guessing, and it is explicit that the unbounded problems are decidable-but-Ackermannian, with the frontier sitting one line behind the ω check. The one place this discipline currently fails is worth stating plainly: the unbounded-liveness path returns every transition as L0, the same value a genuinely dead transition receives, so "we don't know" and "provably dead" carry the same value — distinguished only by a sibling method-field that the boolean wrappers discard. Making that distinction type-enforced is the outstanding honesty repair the calculus requires. (The bibliography keeps the same discipline: where conventions diverge — the Primer's |P|×|T| incidence against Murata's transpose — the code records which it chose.)

### The certificate is the trust boundary: the heuristic chooses what to try, the proof decides what is believed

This answers the founding design question: *what kind of knowing should the tool produce — a proof, or a good guess?* The codebase's answer, embodied before it was stated, is **both, with a boundary between them.** Because every decider that concludes emits a checkable certificate, and because acceptance requires an actual certificate, the *order* in which deciders are tried cannot affect the answer — only the time to reach it.

This is the **firewall**, and it is the *enabling property* of the whole construction — not its headline. It is worth being exact about its standing, because the precision is the point. As a **theorem** it is a single line: the execution returns a verdict only if some decider produced a certificate that a checker accepted; the policy influences only which deciders run and in what order, never the acceptance predicate; therefore the returned verdict is correct irrespective of the policy. That theorem is a one-line corollary of two mature results — certifying algorithms (McConnell–Mehlhorn–Näher) composed with algorithm selection (Rice; SATzilla) — and it should be presented as such, not as a novelty.

Its *non-trivial content is a precondition*, not the corollary. The theorem covers only the *certifying* fraction of the decider set: a decider returning a bare verdict with no checkable certificate is, in effect, part of the trusted base, and scheduling it with any policy neither protects nor repairs it. The strength of the firewall is exactly that certifying fraction, `f`. And today two arms violate the precondition outright — `is_covered_by_s_components` (`api/net/mod.rs:270`) returns a hardcoded `Some(false)`, and the marked-graph liveness arm (`liveness.rs:107`) returns `Some(false) // todo` — where the theory gives an exact answer. They are trusted-but-wrong. **The real soundness risk in the whole construction is those stubs, not the machine learning.** Making a decider that cannot yet certify return `None` and escalate — so every fast check carries its proof — is therefore not a parallel nicety but the near-term first move: it is the precondition that turns the firewall from a statement into a fact. The evidence calculus and the firewall are one project, and its first step is small and concrete.

A correspondence worth stating carefully, because it is *true* but is no longer the spine. petrivet does have a property Go's learners lack: in AlphaGo a wrong value estimate at a non-terminal leaf costs the game, whereas here a leaf is a proof and a wrong estimate costs milliseconds; the learner sits *outside* the trusted base, where MuZero's learned dynamics model sits inside it. That observation correctly locates the learner relative to the trust boundary. But the design's honest lineage for selection is **SATzilla and Rice**, not AlphaGo, and the kinship is in fact looser than the analogy suggests: petrivet *has* checkable leaves, which makes its selection problem easier than and structurally unlike game-tree search. So the AlphaGo/MuZero framing is dropped as the organizing metaphor and kept only as a precise note about trust-base placement. The selection direction itself — a learned scheduler over the certified deciders — is the **sequel**, not the headline, and is developed in [soundness as a free variable](soundness-as-a-free-variable.md).

#### The portfolio made operational: the cascade as data, and selection as an instrumented policy

To realize the sequel, lift the hardcoded cascade — the `match self.class()` repeated across five sibling files — into data: a `Decider` trait declaring each decider's polarity (proves-yes, proves-no, or exact), its cost class, and its soundness domain, with the `analyze_*` methods becoming a driver that gathers the admissible deciders, runs them in cost order, and accepts the first certificate. The default schedule reproduces today's behavior exactly — but the schedule is now a *parameter*, and the seed of the learned version already exists as a single `if` in the MCC entry, where the structural-versus-default technique tags are a binary record of which of two deciders fired. The learned policy generalizes that one branch into a function of the full structural feature vector.

This is a SATzilla-style algorithm-selection problem: a cost-sensitive ranker over structural features, trained self-supervised on harness logs (the certificate is the label; no oracle is required for training, only as a cross-check), optimizing regret rather than accuracy. It is justified only if a *measured* gap between the single-best and virtual-best solver warrants it — on a small portfolio the gap may be within noise, in which case the hand-ordered cascade is the honest answer and the ML is dead weight. Three things are worth saying plainly about the supporting theory. First, MCC *ranking* is out of scope as a goal: the contest is the crucible (honest, protocol-correct abstention, cross-checked against an oracle) and the labelling source, not a leaderboard to climb. Second, the effective-theory reading — that an intractable local-update substrate can possess a predictive macro-theory when the macro-variables are autonomous and sufficient (Israeli–Goldenfeld; causal states; the information bottleneck) — is offered as *interpretation of why selection might help on a distribution*, and is labelled speculation, never a design justification; whether the chosen features suffice for the hardness label is a measured question, not a theorem. Third, the cellular-automata and computational-functionalism material is borrowed exactly that far and no further.

#### The rungs: rising ambition, constant soundness

The ambition climbs a ladder whose every rung shares one guarantee, and every rung is a horizon gated behind the certificate. **Rung 0** is today's system: the hardcoded per-class cascade, sound and leaving performance wins unrealized. **Rung 1** is a cost-sensitive empirical hardness ranker — gradient-boosted trees over structural features, no deep learning — that predicts the fastest admissible decider; it is sound by the firewall, carries the caveat that *performance* is not automatically monotone (a confident wrong pick can be slow), and is gated on the measured single-best-to-virtual-best gap. **Rung 2** turns the static ranking into an adaptive, preemptive controller — a Pandora's-box index policy, then offline reinforcement learning from harness logs — that learns *when to abandon* a diverging exploration and race the cheap deciders; it needs the one piece of infrastructure the core lacks, a cooperative cancellation token. **Rung 3** promotes *structural reductions* to actions, so the net becomes a shrinking residual and the search is an AND/OR proof-tree over reduced problems. The discipline that keeps it sound: each reduction must itself be *certifying*, with a `lift` that maps a certificate on the reduced net back to one on the original, checked by the *unchanged* original-net checker — so even a *buggy* lift cannot break soundness, it produces a certificate that fails the checker and the search backtracks. This robustness is clean for *existential* witnesses (firing sequences); for compositional/invariant lifts it must be proven per certificate kind, because a buggy interface correction could in principle pass a too-weak check, so trusted lifts are restricted to existential witnesses until that obligation is discharged. The through-line is the certificate: difficulty rises across the rungs while soundness stays constant, and the whole system is self-labeling because the certificate is the training label. (The rungs are developed in [soundness as a free variable](soundness-as-a-free-variable.md).)

### Truth is what survives a quotient: keep only what is invariant under the don't-cares

Beneath the law runs a method. Every structure in the system names a group of "don't-cares" and keeps only the invariant under it. The marking equation forgets firing *order* and keeps the Parikh image — and its necessity-but-not-sufficiency is *exactly* the information that quotient discards, which is why the refinement loop must add back what order-blindness lost.

#### The machine origin is a don't-care: cost lives in a torsor, fitness in the quotient

Absolute timings have no canonical origin — machine speed and load are an arbitrary additive shift on log-cost — so the only physical content is *differences*: the ranking among deciders and their log-ratios, per instance. The cost vectors form a bundle of torsors indexed by the net's features; a *schedule* (today's cascade, a future learned policy) is a choice of origin, a section of that bundle, recorded *alongside* the data and never baked into it. The resulting discipline is concrete: persist the raw fibres tagged with their run context, treat them as non-comparable across machines except through differential invariants, prefer machine-invariant counters (states explored, LP solves) over wall-time, and phrase every regression test as a differential assertion against a committed baseline section — so the test is portable to a slower CI machine precisely because it lives in the ratios. The cost-sensitive regret a ranker would minimize is itself a difference, origin-free — the torsor-quotient quantity made into a training objective.

#### Observe, never act: the harness and the one-way dependency

The substrate that produces these differential measurements is a separate observability domain, and its single discipline is that the dependency points *only toward the core*: the observer depends on petrivet; nothing in petrivet depends on the observer; the core does not know it is being measured. It observes without certifying and without scheduling — it records *whether* a check passed, once such a check exists, but does not implement one, and it runs deciders to measure them without choosing them for production answers. It is at once a self-test and the future training set. Its always-on **soundness sentinel** — every accepted verdict with a known oracle must agree — is exactly what catches the two trusted-but-wrong stubs; it is the firewall's precondition turned into a live regression test. Phase one needs no core change at all, reading the public method tags; a later, optional, additive seam exposes the individual deciders for per-decider timing. The whole contribution is safe to add and easy to remove because it is a pure downstream observer, and it is deliberately separated from the core algorithm work.

### Specify the interface before implementing it: the blueprint is committed ahead of the implementation

The deepest fact about the whole construction is a single file. `literature.rs` contains no executable code — only the theorems of Murata, Best–Devillers, and Esparza, each doc-linked to the function that implements it. It binds a published theorem and a Rust function so they reference each other; it *is* the README's motto, "bringing Petri net theory and application together," enacted literally as a seam in the source. And it deep-links to a layer that does not yet exist — `compute_invariants`, an `Invariants` type, an `SComponent`, a `crate::model` carrying evidence types — names that resolve to nothing. This is not a flaw; it is the most legible fact in the repository, because it makes *future potential* answerable rather than projective. The gap between the names the file invokes and the names that compile is a precise, self-authored map of the planned-but-unimplemented: the architecture has written its intentions in advance. Every abstraction the planned system would need has a pointer in that file *today* — which is why the planned work is completion, not invention, and why the seams were cut with the next generality already in mind.

## The four principles, summarized

> **1 — SUBSTRATE.** A transition affects only its preset and its postset; the net is one matrix, C = Post − Pre. The whole's hardness is not localized in any single transition.
>
> **2 — COMPLETION.** Rather than walk the state space, complete it. Omega is infinity made into a type; the kernel is the matrix completed into its conservation laws. A finite witness stands in for the infinite fact.
>
> **3 — STRUCTURAL CLASS.** Choose by shape before computing. One closure answers a question about every subset at once; on free-choice nets the over-approximations become exact.
>
> **4 — EPISTEMIC LAW.** Compute the reason, not just the answer — a finite witness anyone can recheck. Then let a heuristic choose what to try; only the checked witness is believed. "Cannot yet decide" is an accurate result, not a failure.
>
> **What they converge on.** Not a quantity to compute but a thing to trust and a thing to measure: a **certificate** any third party can re-check (the stone, the entire trusted base reduced to a small checker), and a **coverage** fraction — `f_struct` and `f`, measured on the real corpus — that says how far the cheap, checkable tier reaches, made safe by the **firewall** that keeps soundness independent of how the tier is scheduled.

Read in sequence: locality makes the question hard; completion makes it answerable without walking the trajectory; the right structural shape makes whole classes of it exact; and the certificate makes it safe to reach for those answers with a heuristic and still be correct — converging not on a number the architecture was built to compute, but on a proof anyone can check and a coverage anyone can falsify.

*Provenance, stated explicitly: Principles 1–3, and the proof-carrying, decidability, and claim-honesty aspects of Principle 4, describe the implemented code — verifiable line by line, down to the two `Some(false)` stubs and the L0 hazard. The certificate calculus and external checker, the WSTS lift, the extracted exact Farkas certificate, the cluster quotient, the certifying portfolio and its rungs, the differential-measurement harness, and the factorization residuals are proposed rather than implemented — offered, not asserted. The certificate-and-checker is the near-term spine; the learned ladder and the residuals are the sequel and the horizon. The blueprint for nearly all of it is already in `literature.rs`. The authoritative engineering plan is the project's [BACKLOG.md](../../BACKLOG.md); these essays support it.*
