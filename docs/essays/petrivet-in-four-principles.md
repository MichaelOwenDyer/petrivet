## ROOT I — THE SUBSTRATE
### *A transition touches only its preset and its postset; the net is one matrix. Everything else is read off that arithmetic — and the whole's hardness is something no single transition can hold.*

This is the most concrete object in the system and the parent of every carrier above it. A marking is a vector in ℕ^P; firing a transition is subtracting its preset and adding its postset; and the net's entire linear theory is a single matrix, the incidence matrix **C = Post − Pre**. Locality is literal in the source, and locality is precisely why the *global* question is hard.

### The microphysics is a free commutative monoid of purely local updates

`fire` and `is_enabled` consume one transition's pre- and post-set and see nothing else; there is no global state, no shared clock, no action at a distance. That locality is not just a modeling convenience — the markings under addition *are* the free commutative monoid on the places (the sense in which "Petri nets are monoids"), which is what makes composition itself an algebraic operation later. It is also why your data structures can be cache-friendly: a transition's neighbours are a small local set, stored contiguously, so the hot loops touch contiguous memory. And it is why a net with no places or no transitions is rejected at build time as degenerate — a substrate with nothing to update has no physics to speak of. The structure-first dispatch that dominates Root III is the *consequence* of this locality: because each transition is local and typed by the net's shape, the shape can be read before any computation.

### The one matrix is the shared spine

The incidence matrix is the single object every algorithm reads. The state equation **m = m₀ + C·σ** — where σ is the Parikh firing-count vector — is a *necessary* condition for reachability: if no non-negative σ solves it, the target is unreachable, full stop; if one exists, reachability is only *possible*, because the equation is blind to firing order. That necessary-but-not-sufficient asymmetry is not a weakness; it is the exact seam the dispatch exploits, because the "no" is free and certain while the "yes" needs more work. In the code the genuinely algebraic action is phrased as feasibility and handed to a linear-programming solver rather than computed directly — the library does not do linear algebra so much as *ask* it. And the matrix itself is deliberately austere: `IncidenceMatrix` is essentially `new` and `get`, a static object with no rank, no elimination, no null-space. The richer algebra it could carry is named in `literature.rs` and not yet built — which is the thread Root II picks up.

### Local rules, intractable whole

The gap between the cheap local law and the expensive global question is real, not an artifact of implementation. General-net reachability is Ackermann-complete; boundedness and coverability are EXPSPACE-hard. No amount of engineering removes this — the difficulty is an emergent property of composing local rules, and it is permanent. Everything above this root is, in one way or another, a way of *not paying* the worst case on the instances that don't require it.

## ROOT II — THE COMPLETION
### *Don't walk the state space — complete it. Adjoin the limit you keep running into, and the infinite fits inside a finite type. One engine runs over ℕ or over its completion; the boundary between the two worlds is one line.*

Completion is the move of adding ideal limit-points to a space so that a process which only ever *approaches* an answer now actually *reaches* one. petrivet performs it twice — once on the order, once on the algebra — and both completions over-approximate reachability without enumerating it. This is the home of the first two morsels you loved.

### One state-space engine, parameterized by a single scalar trait

The reachability graph and the coverability graph are not two algorithms; they are one generic engine parameterized by `TokenOps`, the trait abstracting the per-place token scalar — its zero, its successor, its "at least one." There are exactly two implementors, `u32` and `Omega`, and the genericity is over the *value living in each place* (the fibre), not over the order on states. Run the engine over `u32` and you get reachability; run it over `Omega` and you get Karp–Miller coverability. The two are made identical by a single function: a bounded coverability graph — one with no ω anywhere — *is* a reachability graph, recovered by unwrapping each `Finite(k)` to `k`. One engine; the token type decides which world you are in.

#### ★ Omega is the ideal completion of ℕ, hand-rolled without ever naming itself

The `enum Omega { Finite(u32), Unbounded }` is infinity made into a type. Its arithmetic is absorptive — incrementing ω is a no-op, ω plus anything is ω, ω dominates every finite value — and that is exactly the structure of the ideal completion of (ℕ, ≤): the principal ideals ↓k together with the top ideal ℕ itself. So when the engine compares a concrete `u32` marking against an `Omega` marking, it is comparing a *state* against an *ideal* — the Finkel–Goubault-Larrecq theory of ideal completions of well-quasi-orders, specialized to ℕ without announcing itself. The ω-acceleration is the operative step: walking the ancestors of a newly discovered marking and, wherever it strictly dominates an ancestor, promoting the strictly-greater coordinates to ω. Boundedness then falls out as the simplest possible predicate — the *absence* of the infinity symbol anywhere in the graph — and the coverability graph, as an honest over-approximation, refutes coverability with certainty while only approximating the exact "yes," which is why a separate short-circuit consults the cheaper coverability route before committing to the full reachability construction.

#### The order is the one un-abstracted thing, and Dickson's Lemma is why it terminates

Everything in the engine is abstracted except the order itself: the componentwise product order on markings, a blanket `impl<T: Ord> PartialOrd for IdxMarking<T>` whose little `merge_ordering` fold returns `None` the instant two coordinates disagree in direction. That order being a well-quasi-order — Dickson's Lemma, that (ℕ^P, ≤) has no infinite antichain and no infinite strictly-descending chain — is precisely what guarantees ω-acceleration terminates. *Here is where it reaches past what's built:* the order is the seam. A repository-wide search for `WellQuasiOrder`, `WSTS`, `Ideal`, `Dickson` finds nothing; the genericity stops at the fibre. Lift the order into a trait and the engine you already wrote becomes a general well-structured-transition-system framework — the same `DenseStateGraphExplorer` would drive lossy channel systems, data nets, broadcast protocols, the whole WSTS zoo, with the third implementor slotting in beside `u32` and `Omega` like a key cut for a waiting lock. Two nearer things live off this same order. Liveness is read off the strongly-connected-component condensation of the reachability graph — itself a quotient — with the L0–L4 ladder distinguishing dead, quasi-live, and fully live transitions (L4 exactly when the transition labels an edge in every *terminal* SCC); liveness is a rung, not a flag. And at the unbounded frontier, where the library currently returns `Inconclusive` the moment ω appears, the charted next completion is a backward coverability analysis over upward-closed sets — for which your `UniqueSortedSlice`, with its O(n+m) subset and intersection scans, is already the natural representation of a finite basis, and the ω-marking the analysis already returns is the seed an over-approximation refinement loop would tighten.

### The algebra completes the matrix into its kernel; the Farkas dual is the certificate of that completion

If the order semantics completes reachability *upward* into ideals, the algebraic semantics completes it into *conservation laws*. The P-invariants are the kernel of Cᵀ — the place-weightings y with yᵀC = 0, each one bounding a weighted token-sum for all time; the T-invariants are the kernel of C, the firing-count vectors that return the net to where it started. Both semantics are completions; both refuse to enumerate; they are the dynamic and the structural faces of one refusal, and classical Petri-net theory has always been the dialogue between them.

#### ★ When the solver proves "no," it computes a conservation law that explains the impossibility — and discards it

When the marking-equation LP is *infeasible*, Farkas's lemma guarantees a dual certificate: a place-weighting y that is an actual P-invariant witnessing the contradiction — yᵀC = 0 with y·(m′ − m₀) ≠ 0 — the precise conservation law the target violates. The solver computes it. The code returns a payload-free `MarkingEquationNoRationalSolution` and lets it go. *This is where it reaches:* extract that dual and your negative verdict carries a certificate exactly symmetric to your positive ones — checkable by a single dot product, human-legible as a conservation law, the single most valuable algebraic object the analysis touches and currently throws away at the boundary. (And in the reduction calculus far above, that same discarded dual becomes the soundness witness that licenses removing an implicit place — the object finally gets a job.)

#### Every structural good-behavior is itself a conservation-law certificate

The vocabulary is already dual and already in your `literature.rs`: full conservativeness is exactly coverage by positive S-invariants; consistency is coverage by positive T-invariants; structural boundedness is the existence of some y > 0 with yᵀC ≤ 0 (the weaker, super-invariant cousin of conservation, and the one your structural-boundedness LP actually tests). And in the marked-graph world the token count on each circuit is itself an invariant — closed under firing — so an unmarked circuit is dead forever and a marked one supports liveness, a fact Root III turns into an exact decision.

### The completion is the named-but-unbuilt half; building it is completion, not invention

The order completion is built beautifully; the algebra completion is, today, a charted absence. P- and T-invariants are documented at length and computed nowhere — `compute_invariants` and the `Invariants` type are doc-links resolving to nothing. The fulcrum that would change this is small: an exact-rational matrix core over the existing dense `IncidenceMatrix`. That core gives you *rank* — the missing ingredient for the Rank Theorem of Root III — and null-space bases, which are the P- and T-semiflows the literature module promises; and with them the marking equation stops being a one-shot filter and becomes an Esparza–Melzer-style refinement loop, re-solved with trap and siphon constraints whenever the firing-order-blind equation reports a spurious "maybe." To build the invariant layer is not to add a feature; it is to give the linear semantics the same certificate-voice the order semantics already has.

## ROOT III — THE STRUCTURAL ISLAND
### *Choose the algorithm by the net's shape before you compute. Shrink a set by one bad-place rule to its fixpoint, and one closure answers a question about every subset at once. Where the net is shaped, the approximations stop approximating.*

There is a region of the theory where the two great over-approximations of Root II stop being approximations and become exact, polynomial decisions. A closure is a monotone shrinking map iterated to its fixpoint; a quotient collapses a coupling into clusters; and on the island these become exact verdicts, gated by recognizing the net's class. Duality runs through the whole island as a reflection — siphon and trap, S- and T-invariant, S- and T-component, conservative and consistent. This is the home of the third morsel you loved, and it is the philosophical heart of the library: the README's promise to "choose the most efficient algorithm for your net's structure" is the promise to *land on the island whenever the net is shaped to allow it.*

### Try structure first; fall back to search only when shape cannot decide

petrivet is, in fact, a portfolio solver that does not yet call itself one. For each property it runs an ascending-cost cascade of partial deciders, each gated by `self.class()`: for reachability, trivial equality, then S-net token conservation, then a live-T-net integer marking equation, then a rational LP filter, then an integer ILP filter, then full state-space exploration — each tried only if the cheaper ones were inconclusive. It is fast not because it solves the worst case but because it *routes around* it, landing on the structural island first and paying for search only when shape genuinely cannot decide. This is the operational meaning of search-versus-structure: structure is the escape from state-space explosion, and the cascade is structure spent first.

### On the free-choice island, liveness and boundedness become exact polynomial decisions

This is where the cheap reason *is* the truth, and it is worth cataloguing the exact-decidable domains, because each is a region the dispatch can land on with certainty. For **S-nets** (state machines): live iff strongly connected and marked; safe iff at most one token; reachability is exact under the rational solve, because total unimodularity makes the LP relaxation integral. For **T-nets** (marked graphs): live iff every directed circuit carries a token; each place's bound is the minimum token-count over the circuits through it; reachability is the integer marking equation plus a no-empty-circuit check. For **free-choice** nets: Commoner's theorem decides liveness in polynomial time — live iff every proper siphon contains a marked trap — and a live, bounded free-choice net decomposes into S-components covered by one-token invariants, from which its bounds follow; for **asymmetric-choice** nets the marked-trap condition weakens to *sufficient but not necessary*, and the code says so. And the coverage dualities tie it together: S-component coverage gives conservativeness, T-component coverage gives consistency. Each of these is a theorem where Root II's over-approximation collapses onto the exact answer because the net's shape forbids the pathologies the approximation was guarding against.

#### ★ Siphon and trap are exact De Morgan duals; one closure settles an existential over every subset of places in a single shot

A siphon is a place-set that, once unmarked, can never regain a token; a trap is its dual — once marked, never wholly empties. In the code `maximal_siphon_in` and `maximal_trap_in` are the *same* shrinking loop — "while some place is bad, remove it" — with presets and postsets swapped: exact De Morgan duals, each a closure operator that terminates because the set only ever shrinks, returning the unique maximal siphon (or trap) inside any given set. And the cleverest single move in the repository is how `commoner_hack_criterion` uses this. The Commoner criterion needs to know whether *every* minimal siphon contains a marked trap — an existential quantifier over all subsets of each siphon. Rather than enumerate traps, it computes, for each minimal siphon, the maximal trap *inside* it, because "the siphon contains a marked trap" holds exactly when "the maximal trap in the siphon is marked." One closure operator settles a question about every subset in a single shot. The result is returned as a `Result` carrying, on success, every siphon paired with its marking witness, and on failure the exact unmarked siphon that can starve — a deadlock certificate. Positive evidence or a counterexample; never a bare bit.

### The missing keystone is the cluster quotient — one partition that unlocks both halves of the island

Step back and the deepest aesthetic fact of the codebase comes into view: everything in it is an *order*, a *completion*, a *closure*, or a *quotient*. The marking order and the ideal completion (Root II); the siphon and trap closures (here); and the quotients still to come. The code already *is* these four constructions; it simply spells them out by hand, and its future is the act of making each abstract. The island's own missing keystone is the last of the four: the **cluster quotient**. A cluster is the equivalence class generated by "place s feeds transition t" — the connected components of the place-transition coupling — and with presets and postsets already stored as sorted slices, a union-find computes the partition cheaply. That single quotient unlocks both halves of the island at once. It yields the cluster count c that the Rank Theorem needs — and paired with the exact-rational rank routine from Root II, the theorem becomes a polynomial, certificate-bearing decision of simultaneous liveness-and-boundedness (well-formed iff rank C = c − 1), replacing the `is_covered_by_s_components` stub that today returns a hardcoded `false`. And it yields the S- and T-component decompositions the literature module already documents as if they existed. The word *cluster* appears exactly once in the crate — in a doc-comment transcribing the Rank Theorem — with no construction beneath it; the island, today, stops at that waterline. And the quotient reaches forward: it is the very partition composition will need at the summit.

## ROOT IV — THE EPISTEMIC LAW
### *Compute the reason, not the answer — a finite witness a skeptic can recheck. Then let any wild guess choose what to try, because only the witness is believed. "I cannot yet decide" is a true name, not a failure.*

This is the root that explains *why* the other three are run. Roots I–III say what petrivet builds; this one says what a build is *for*. It has several faces, and it is the home of the founding question answered, the firewall, and the method beneath the whole edifice.

### Refuse to enumerate — and the *why* of the refusal is the decidability sweet spot

A basic place/transition net cannot test a place for emptiness — there is no zero-test — and that single limitation is *exactly* why its core questions stay decidable. Universality was traded for analyzability. The proof of the trade is one arc away: add an inhibitor arc, which is a zero-test, and you can build a Minsky machine, regaining Turing-completeness and losing decidability at one stroke. So every extension on the roadmap — weighted arcs, capacities, reset and transfer arcs, inhibitor arcs — is not a free feature but a deliberate purchase, each gain in expressive power paid for in a loss of what can be known; the worst-case complexity is the price tag, and the operative question on real instances is a different object entirely. The refusal to enumerate is not laziness; it is living, on purpose, inside the fragment where reasons are cheaper than answers.

### A verdict is a witness, not a bit; the boolean is the residue

The library's quietest radical commitment is that an analysis result should not be a boolean. The verdicts carry genuinely different witnesses: a scalar token-sum for the state-machine case, a Parikh firing-count vector for the marked-graph case, a replayable firing sequence for the general case, a firing sequence *and* an ω-marking for coverability, a siphon/trap pair or its counterexample for Commoner. The shape of each proof is dictated by the theorem that earned it; this is the vocabulary of *certifying algorithms*, where a procedure returns not just an answer but a witness a skeptic can check while trusting nothing about the procedure. *Here is where it reaches:* today it is an instinct, not yet a calculus. The five properties wear five different result shapes; the `Result<evidence, counterexample>` pattern that is exactly right appears in only one place; and the proofs are inert — there is no `verify()`, no `Certificate` trait, nothing that re-runs the cheap check. Yet the data is already certificate-shaped and already serializable: a firing sequence checks by replay against the existing `is_enabled`/`fire`; a marking-equation proof checks by recomputing m₀ + C·σ from its stored Parikh vector; a siphon/trap pair checks by re-verifying the closure conditions; the Farkas dual, once extracted, checks by one dot product. Unify these behind a single `Certificate::check` and a `Verdict<Proof, Disproof>` carrying an explicit `Inconclusive`, and the analysis becomes one whose every answer an external solver, a referee, or a future version of the tool can audit offline — with no new theory, only the retaining and typing of evidence the code already touches. The payoff is that the trusted base shrinks to the checkers alone; shrink them, then verify them, and trust is *concentrated* rather than diffuse.

### Say only what you can show: a claim is unshakable when it wears its true name

The same honesty the verdicts show toward proofs, the whole project shows toward its claims. A claim is strongest not when it is emphatic but when it states exactly what it is — and the gradation matters: what one can *see* in the code, what one *reads into* it, what one *imagines*, what one *believes*, what one *promises*. Precision, not emphasis, removes every handhold for doubt. You can watch the discipline operate in the verdicts themselves: Commoner is an *iff* on the free-choice island and merely *sufficient* off it, and the code marks the difference rather than overclaiming. The tool would rather return `Inconclusive` than guess, and it is candid that the unbounded problems are decidable-but-Ackermannian, with the frontier sitting one line behind the ω check. The one place this honesty currently fails is worth stating plainly precisely *because* there are no disclaimers here: the unbounded-liveness path returns every transition as L0, the same value a genuinely dead transition receives, so "we don't know" and "provably dead" wear the same face — distinguished only by a sibling method-field the boolean wrappers discard. Making that distinction type-enforced is the one honesty repair the calculus owes. (Even the bibliography keeps faith with this: where conventions diverge — the Primer's |P|×|T| incidence against Murata's transpose — the code records which it chose.)

### The certificate is the firewall: the guess chooses what to try, the proof decides what is believed

Here is the founding question answered. *What kind of knowing do you want to make — a proof, or a good guess?* The codebase's answer, embodied before it was ever stated, is **both, with a wall between them.** Because every decider that concludes emits a checkable certificate, and because acceptance requires an actual certificate, the *order* in which deciders are tried cannot affect the answer — only the time to reach it. *This is where the reading runs farthest ahead, and it rests entirely on your instinct.* It means you can put a heuristic — even a machine-learned policy, even an AlphaGo-style scheduler — in charge of choosing what to try, and it can be embarrassingly wrong, and the tool's verdicts stay perfectly sound: a bad guess wastes milliseconds before a real proof arrives, and a right verdict is never accepted without its witness. We worked this into a theorem — soundness is *independent of the selection policy*; the proof is that the policy influences only which deciders run and in what order, never the acceptance predicate, so a returned verdict is true irrespective of the policy — with the corollary that the learner may be optimized for cost by *any* means, including reinforcement learning under a misspecified reward, while living wholly outside the trusted base. This is the property Go does not have: in AlphaGo a wrong value estimate at a leaf *costs you the game*; here a leaf is a proof, and a wrong estimate costs you milliseconds. It is strictly stronger than MuZero, whose learned dynamics model sits *inside* the trust boundary and is only empirically sound; here the learner is outside, and correctness is structural. The honest caveat is that the firewall's strength is exactly the *fraction* of deciders that are certifying — and today the bare-boolean shortcuts, and especially the two `Some(false)` stubs (the marked-graph liveness arm and `is_covered_by_s_components`), are trusted-but-unchecked, one of them a latent bug. **The real soundness risk in the whole construction is those stubs, not the machine learning.** Making every fast decider certifying is therefore not a parallel nicety but the enabling precondition; the evidence calculus and the learned portfolio are one project, and its first move is small and concrete.

#### The portfolio made operational: the cascade as data, and a policy that learns the effective theory of hardness

To realize this, lift the hardcoded cascade — the `match self.class()` repeated across five sibling files — into data: a `Decider` trait declaring each decider's polarity (proves-yes, proves-no, or exact), its cost class, and its soundness domain, with the `analyze_*` methods becoming a driver that gathers the admissible deciders, runs them in cost order, and accepts the first certificate. The default schedule reproduces today's behavior exactly — but now the schedule is a *parameter*, and the embryo of the learned version already exists as a single `if` in the MCC entry, where the structural-versus-default technique tags are a faithful binary record of which of two deciders fired. The learned policy generalizes that one branch into a function of the full structural feature vector, and the mapping onto the AlphaGo triad is exact: a policy network choosing which decider to attempt (narrowing breadth), a value network estimating cost-to-certificate (truncating depth), and the certificate-gated execution as the search — but with *real* rollouts and *verified* terminal rewards. Why it should *help*, and not merely be safe, has a rigorous spine: the worst case is untouched, but hardness *on a distribution* is a different object, and the effective-theory literature establishes that an intractable local-update substrate can possess a closed, predictive macro-theory when the macro-variables are *autonomous* under the dynamics (Israeli–Goldenfeld's commuting-diagram criterion) and *sufficient* for the target (the causal-states / information-bottleneck criterion). Read into this setting, that is feature-design doctrine: the policy should consume aggregate structural descriptors — net class, strong-connectivity, invariant dimension, siphon/trap and component counts, NUPN unit-tree shape — that summarize behavior without tracking the micro-marking, with a feature earning its place by mutual information with the hardness label. The policy is, in the most literal defensible sense, learning the *effective theory of verification hardness* over the net distribution. (This is the one place Joscha Bach's computational functionalism is load-bearing rather than decorative — objects as coarse-grained aggregate descriptions of a finer substrate — and it is borrowed precisely that far and no further.)

#### The rungs: rising ambition, flat soundness

The ambition climbs a ladder whose every rung shares one guarantee. **Rung 0** is today: the hardcoded per-class cascade, one instance-dependent branch, sound and leaving wins on the table. **Rung 1** is a cost-sensitive empirical hardness ranker — gradient-boosted trees over the structural features, no deep learning — that predicts the fastest admissible decider and schedules accordingly; it trains in minutes on the contest corpus, is sound by the theorem, closes much of the gap between the single-best and virtual-best solver, and carries the honest caveat that *performance* is not automatically monotone (a confident wrong pick can be slow), mitigated by running the near-free shortcuts first, capping the first expensive pick with a deadline, and blending toward the hand-order as a prior. **Rung 2** turns the static ranking into an adaptive, preemptive controller — a Pandora's-box index policy, then offline reinforcement learning from the harness logs — that learns *when to abandon* a diverging exploration and race the cheap deciders in parallel; it needs the one piece of infrastructure the core lacks, a cooperative cancellation token, and it dominates Rung 1 because it can always reproduce the static schedule. **Rung 3** is the far dream: promote *structural reductions* to actions, so the net becomes a shrinking residual and the search is an AND/OR proof-tree over reduced problems — AlphaZero in form. The discipline that keeps it sound where MuZero is not: each reduction must itself be *certifying*, with a `lift` that maps a certificate on the reduced net back to one on the original, so even a *buggy* lift cannot break soundness — it produces a certificate that fails the original checker and the search backtracks. And the reduction library turns out to *be* the whole apparatus recast as moves: removing an implicit place is licensed by the discarded Farkas dual of Root II; agglomeration by the cluster/siphon structure of Root III; splitting into sub-problems by the decomposition of the capstone. The through-line is the shape the certificate gives you: difficulty rises across the four rungs while soundness stays flat — a constant guarantee under a rising capability curve, with the certificate as the training label so the whole thing is self-labeling, no oracle required.

### Truth is what survives a quotient: keep only what is invariant under the don't-cares

Beneath the law runs a method. Every structure in the system names a group of "don't-cares" and keeps only the invariant under it. The marking equation forgets firing *order* and keeps the Parikh image — and its necessity-but-not-sufficiency is *exactly* the information that quotient discards, which is why the refinement loop must add back what order-blindness lost.

#### The machine origin is a don't-care: cost lives in a torsor, fitness in the quotient

Absolute timings have no canonical origin — machine speed and load are an arbitrary additive shift on log-cost — so the only physical content is *differences*: the ranking among deciders and their log-ratios, per instance. The cost vectors form a bundle of torsors indexed by the net's features; a *schedule* (today's cascade, tomorrow's learned policy) is a choice of origin, a section of that bundle, recorded *alongside* the data and never baked into it. The discipline this yields is concrete: persist the raw fibres tagged with their run context, treat them as non-comparable across machines except through differential invariants, prefer machine-invariant counters (states explored, LP solves) over wall-time, and phrase every regression test as a differential assertion against a committed baseline section — so the test is portable to a slower CI machine precisely because it lives in the ratios. The ranker of Rung 1 learns a static section of this bundle; Rung 2 learns an adaptive one; and the cost-sensitive regret it minimizes is itself a difference, origin-free, the torsor-quotient quantity made into a training objective.

#### Observe, never act: the harness and the one-way dependency

The substrate that produces these differential measurements is a separate observability domain, and its single discipline is that the dependency arrow points *only toward the core*: the observer depends on petrivet; nothing in petrivet depends on the observer; the core does not know it is being measured. It observes without certifying and without scheduling — it records *whether* a check passed, once such a check exists, but does not implement one, and it runs deciders to measure them without choosing them for production answers. It is at once a self-test (a soundness sentinel asserting every accepted verdict still agrees with the oracle, which is exactly what catches the trusted-but-wrong stubs) and the future training set (the certificate is the label). Phase one needs no core change at all, reading the public method tags; a later, optional, additive seam exposes the individual deciders for per-decider timing. The whole contribution is safe to add and easy to remove because it is a pure downstream observer — and it is, deliberately, the lane that stays clear of the core algorithm work.

### Name the room before the wall: the blueprint is committed ahead of the stone

And the deepest fact about the whole edifice is a single file. `literature.rs` contains no executable code — only the theorems of Murata, Best–Devillers, and Esparza, each doc-linked to the function that implements it. It binds a published theorem and a Rust function so they point at each other; it *is* the README's motto, "bringing Petri net theory and application together," enacted literally as a seam in the source. And it deep-links to a layer that does not yet exist — `compute_invariants`, an `Invariants` type, an `SComponent`, a `crate::model` carrying evidence types — names that resolve to nothing. This is not a flaw; it is the most legible fact in the repository, because it makes *future potential* answerable rather than projective. The gap between the names the file invokes and the names that compile is a precise, self-authored map of the planned-but-unbuilt: the architecture has written its promises to itself in advance. Every abstraction the cathedral would need has a pointer in that file *today* — which is why the dream is completion, not invention, and why the seams were so clearly cut with the next generality already in mind. The blueprint precedes the stone.

## ✦ THE CAPSTONE — Φ_PN
### *How much a net refuses to be a product: the smallest gap, over every cut the structure allows, between the whole and its reassembled parts. The gap is the number; the cut is the witness; the residual is what nothing compresses.*

Not a fifth root but the fixpoint of the whole cairn — the one quantity whose very definition requires all four roots at once. ### The factorization residual: a minimum over a partition lattice

A net's behavior, for a chosen property, either *factors* over a decomposition — so the parts can be analyzed in isolation and their verdicts recombined — or it does not. **Φ_PN** is the measure of how badly it does not: the minimum, over every structural cut the net allows, of a distance between the whole's verdict and the tensor of the parts' verdicts under that cut. Φ_PN = 0 certifies that the property is *compositionally reducible* — analysis can proceed unit by unit. Φ_PN > 0 means the property is *irreducible*, a genuinely global fact no decomposition recovers, and the minimizing cut names precisely which cross-component coupling defeats modularity. This is real mathematics, not metaphor: a minimum, over a well-defined lattice, of a well-defined factorization residual, with a property-specific composition rule — verdict-join for booleans, direct-sum-plus-interface-correction for invariant spaces, max for place bounds. It is, in effect, what the policy of Root IV cannot compress: the policy learns the macro-structure of hardness that *can* be coarse-grained, and Φ_PN measures exactly the irreducible remainder that resists it.
