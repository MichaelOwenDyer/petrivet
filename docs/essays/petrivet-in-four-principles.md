# petrivet in Four Principles

> Status: technical overview. This document distills the library's architecture into four principles that converge on a single quantity. It distinguishes throughout between what the code currently implements (verifiable line by line) and where the analysis is proposed rather than implemented. Companion to the other essays in this folder.

Every type, algorithm, and outstanding `// todo` in the library descends from four principles that converge on a single quantity. Each principle is stated below, then developed downward through its intermediate ideas to the concrete details anchored in the code. Principles 1–3, together with the proof-carrying, decidability, and claim-honesty aspects of Principle 4, describe the implemented architecture and can be checked against the source directly. The remainder — the generalizations, the certificate calculus, the learned portfolio, and the capstone quantity — is proposed rather than implemented, and is marked as such throughout.

---

## Principle 1: The substrate

*A transition affects only its preset and its postset; the net is a single matrix. The remaining theory is read off that arithmetic, and the global hardness of the net is not localized in any single transition.*

This is the most concrete object in the system and the parent of every structure built above it. A marking is a vector in ℕ^P; firing a transition subtracts its preset and adds its postset; and the net's entire linear theory is a single matrix, the incidence matrix **C = Post − Pre**. Locality is explicit in the source, and locality is precisely why the *global* question is hard.

### The microphysics is a free commutative monoid of purely local updates

`fire` and `is_enabled` consume one transition's pre- and post-set and reference nothing else; there is no global state, no shared clock, and no nonlocal interaction. This locality is not only a modeling convenience: the markings under addition form the free commutative monoid on the places (the sense in which "Petri nets are monoids"), which is what makes composition itself an algebraic operation later. It also allows the data structures to be cache-friendly: a transition's neighbours form a small local set, stored contiguously, so the hot loops touch contiguous memory. It is also why a net with no places or no transitions is rejected at build time as degenerate — a substrate with nothing to update has no associated dynamics. The structure-first dispatch that dominates Principle 3 is a consequence of this locality: because each transition is local and typed by the net's shape, the shape can be read before any computation.

### The incidence matrix is the shared spine

The incidence matrix is the single object every algorithm reads. The state equation **m = m₀ + C·σ** — where σ is the Parikh firing-count vector — is a *necessary* condition for reachability: if no non-negative σ solves it, the target is unreachable; if one exists, reachability is only *possible*, because the equation is blind to firing order. This necessary-but-not-sufficient asymmetry is not a weakness; it is the seam the dispatch exploits, because the "no" answer is cheap and certain while the "yes" answer requires further work. In the code the algebraic step is phrased as a feasibility query handed to a linear-programming solver rather than computed directly; the library does not perform the linear algebra so much as delegate it. The matrix itself is deliberately minimal: `IncidenceMatrix` is essentially `new` and `get`, a static object with no rank, no elimination, and no null-space computation. The richer algebra it could carry is named in `literature.rs` and not yet implemented — the thread Principle 2 develops.

### Local rules, intractable whole

The gap between the cheap local law and the expensive global question is genuine, not an artifact of implementation. General-net reachability is Ackermann-complete; boundedness and coverability are EXPSPACE-hard. No amount of engineering removes this — the difficulty is an emergent property of composing local rules, and it is permanent. Everything above this principle is, in one way or another, a means of *avoiding* the worst case on the instances that do not require it.

## Principle 2: The completion

*Rather than enumerate the state space, complete it. Adjoin the limit point the process repeatedly approaches, and the infinite fits inside a finite type. One engine runs over ℕ or over its completion; the boundary between the two is a single line of code.*

Completion is the operation of adding ideal limit-points to a space so that a process which only ever *approaches* an answer now *reaches* one. petrivet performs it twice — once on the order, once on the algebra — and both completions over-approximate reachability without enumerating it.

### One state-space engine, parameterized by a single scalar trait

The reachability graph and the coverability graph are not two algorithms; they are one generic engine parameterized by `TokenOps`, the trait abstracting the per-place token scalar — its zero, its successor, its "at least one" predicate. There are exactly two implementors, `u32` and `Omega`, and the genericity is over the *value living in each place* (the fibre), not over the order on states. Running the engine over `u32` yields reachability; running it over `Omega` yields Karp–Miller coverability. The two are made identical by a single function: a bounded coverability graph — one with no ω anywhere — *is* a reachability graph, recovered by unwrapping each `Finite(k)` to `k`. One engine; the token type determines which case applies.

#### Omega as the ideal completion of ℕ

The `enum Omega { Finite(u32), Unbounded }` represents infinity as a type. Its arithmetic is absorptive — incrementing ω is a no-op, ω plus anything is ω, and ω dominates every finite value — which is exactly the structure of the ideal completion of (ℕ, ≤): the principal ideals ↓k together with the top ideal ℕ itself. So when the engine compares a concrete `u32` marking against an `Omega` marking, it is comparing a *state* against an *ideal* — the Finkel–Goubault-Larrecq theory of ideal completions of well-quasi-orders, specialized to ℕ. The ω-acceleration is the operative step: it walks the ancestors of a newly discovered marking and, wherever the new marking strictly dominates an ancestor, promotes the strictly-greater coordinates to ω. Boundedness then reduces to the simplest predicate — the *absence* of the infinity symbol anywhere in the graph — and the coverability graph, as an over-approximation, refutes coverability with certainty while only approximating the exact "yes." This is why a separate short-circuit consults the cheaper coverability route before committing to the full reachability construction.

#### The order is the one un-abstracted component, and Dickson's Lemma is why it terminates

Everything in the engine is abstracted except the order itself: the componentwise product order on markings, a blanket `impl<T: Ord> PartialOrd for IdxMarking<T>` whose `merge_ordering` fold returns `None` the moment two coordinates disagree in direction. That order being a well-quasi-order — Dickson's Lemma, that (ℕ^P, ≤) has no infinite antichain and no infinite strictly-descending chain — is precisely what guarantees ω-acceleration terminates.

*This is where the design is proposed rather than implemented:* the order is the natural generalization seam. A repository-wide search for `WellQuasiOrder`, `WSTS`, `Ideal`, or `Dickson` finds nothing; the genericity stops at the fibre. Lifting the order into a trait would turn the existing engine into a general well-structured-transition-system framework — the same `DenseStateGraphExplorer` would drive lossy channel systems, data nets, broadcast protocols, and the wider WSTS class, with a third implementor slotting in beside `u32` and `Omega`.

Two nearer extensions build on this same order. Liveness is read off the strongly-connected-component condensation of the reachability graph — itself a quotient — with the L0–L4 ladder distinguishing dead, quasi-live, and fully live transitions (L4 exactly when the transition labels an edge in every *terminal* SCC); liveness is a graded property, not a boolean flag. And at the unbounded frontier, where the library currently returns `Inconclusive` the moment ω appears, the proposed next completion is a backward coverability analysis over upward-closed sets — for which `UniqueSortedSlice`, with its O(n+m) subset and intersection scans, is already the natural representation of a finite basis, and the ω-marking the analysis already returns is the seed an over-approximation refinement loop would tighten.

### The algebra completes the matrix into its kernel; the Farkas dual is the certificate of that completion

If the order semantics completes reachability *upward* into ideals, the algebraic semantics completes it into *conservation laws*. The P-invariants are the kernel of Cᵀ — the place-weightings y with yᵀC = 0, each one bounding a weighted token-sum for all time; the T-invariants are the kernel of C, the firing-count vectors that return the net to its initial marking. Both semantics are completions; both avoid enumeration; they are the dynamic and structural faces of the same approach, and classical Petri-net theory has always been the dialogue between them.

#### When the solver proves "no," it computes a conservation law that explains the impossibility — and discards it

When the marking-equation LP is *infeasible*, Farkas's lemma guarantees a dual certificate: a place-weighting y that is an actual P-invariant witnessing the contradiction — yᵀC = 0 with y·(m′ − m₀) ≠ 0 — the precise conservation law the target violates. The solver computes it. The code returns a payload-free `MarkingEquationNoRationalSolution` and discards it.

*This is a proposed extension:* extracting that dual would make the negative verdict carry a certificate symmetric to the positive ones — checkable by a single dot product, human-legible as a conservation law, and the most valuable algebraic object the analysis touches yet currently discards at the boundary. (In the reduction calculus discussed under Principle 4, that same discarded dual becomes the soundness witness that licenses removing an implicit place.)

#### Every structural good-behavior property is itself a conservation-law certificate

The vocabulary is already dual and already present in `literature.rs`: full conservativeness is exactly coverage by positive S-invariants; consistency is coverage by positive T-invariants; structural boundedness is the existence of some y > 0 with yᵀC ≤ 0 (the weaker, super-invariant relative of conservation, and the condition the structural-boundedness LP actually tests). In the marked-graph case the token count on each circuit is itself an invariant — closed under firing — so an unmarked circuit is dead permanently and a marked one supports liveness, a fact Principle 3 converts into an exact decision.

### The completion is the named-but-unimplemented half; building it is completion, not invention

The order completion is implemented; the algebra completion is currently a documented absence. P- and T-invariants are documented at length and computed nowhere — `compute_invariants` and the `Invariants` type are doc-links resolving to nothing. The enabling component is small: an exact-rational matrix core over the existing dense `IncidenceMatrix`. That core provides *rank* — the missing ingredient for the Rank Theorem of Principle 3 — and null-space bases, which are the P- and T-semiflows the literature module documents; and with them the marking equation becomes an Esparza–Melzer-style refinement loop, re-solved with trap and siphon constraints whenever the firing-order-blind equation reports a spurious "maybe." Building the invariant layer gives the linear semantics the same certificate-bearing form the order semantics already has.

## Principle 3: The structural class

*Choose the algorithm by the net's shape before computing. Reduce a set by one bad-place rule to its fixpoint, and one closure answers a question about every subset at once. Where the net has the right structure, the over-approximations become exact.*

There is a region of the theory where the two over-approximations of Principle 2 stop being approximations and become exact, polynomial decisions. A closure is a monotone shrinking map iterated to its fixpoint; a quotient collapses a coupling into clusters; and within this region these become exact verdicts, gated by recognizing the net's class. Duality runs through the whole region as a reflection — siphon and trap, S- and T-invariant, S- and T-component, conservative and consistent. This is the philosophical center of the library: the README's promise to "choose the most efficient algorithm for your net's structure" is the promise to use these exact decisions whenever the net's class permits.

### Try structure first; fall back to search only when shape cannot decide

petrivet is in effect a portfolio solver that does not yet describe itself as one. For each property it runs an ascending-cost cascade of partial deciders, each gated by `self.class()`: for reachability, trivial equality, then S-net token conservation, then a live-T-net integer marking equation, then a rational LP filter, then an integer ILP filter, then full state-space exploration — each tried only if the cheaper ones were inconclusive. It is fast not because it solves the worst case but because it routes around it, using the structural decisions first and paying for search only when shape genuinely cannot decide. This is the operational meaning of search-versus-structure: structure is the escape from state-space explosion, and the cascade spends structure first.

### On free-choice nets, liveness and boundedness become exact polynomial decisions

This is where the cheap reason *is* the correct answer, and it is worth cataloguing the exact-decidable classes, because each is a region the dispatch can decide with certainty. For **S-nets** (state machines): live iff strongly connected and marked; safe iff at most one token; reachability is exact under the rational solve, because total unimodularity makes the LP relaxation integral. For **T-nets** (marked graphs): live iff every directed circuit carries a token; each place's bound is the minimum token-count over the circuits through it; reachability is the integer marking equation plus a no-empty-circuit check. For **free-choice** nets: Commoner's theorem decides liveness in polynomial time — live iff every proper siphon contains a marked trap — and a live, bounded free-choice net decomposes into S-components covered by one-token invariants, from which its bounds follow; for **asymmetric-choice** nets the marked-trap condition weakens to *sufficient but not necessary*, and the code notes this. The coverage dualities tie it together: S-component coverage gives conservativeness, T-component coverage gives consistency. Each of these is a theorem where Principle 2's over-approximation collapses onto the exact answer because the net's shape excludes the pathologies the approximation was guarding against.

#### Siphon and trap are exact De Morgan duals; one closure settles an existential over every subset of places in a single computation

A siphon is a place-set that, once unmarked, can never regain a token; a trap is its dual — once marked, it never wholly empties. In the code `maximal_siphon_in` and `maximal_trap_in` are the *same* shrinking loop — "while some place is bad, remove it" — with presets and postsets swapped: exact De Morgan duals, each a closure operator that terminates because the set only ever shrinks, returning the unique maximal siphon (or trap) inside any given set. `commoner_hack_criterion` uses this efficiently. The Commoner criterion needs to determine whether *every* minimal siphon contains a marked trap — an existential quantifier over all subsets of each siphon. Rather than enumerate traps, it computes, for each minimal siphon, the maximal trap *inside* it, because "the siphon contains a marked trap" holds exactly when "the maximal trap in the siphon is marked." One closure operator settles a question about every subset in a single computation. The result is returned as a `Result` carrying, on success, every siphon paired with its marking witness, and on failure the exact unmarked siphon that can starve — a deadlock certificate. Positive evidence or a counterexample, never a bare bit.

### The missing construction is the cluster quotient — one partition that unlocks both halves

A recurring structural fact of the codebase is that every construction in it is an *order*, a *completion*, a *closure*, or a *quotient*: the marking order and the ideal completion (Principle 2); the siphon and trap closures (here); and the quotients still to come. The code already realizes these four constructions; it spells them out by hand, and its planned development is to make each abstract. The missing construction here is the last of the four: the **cluster quotient**. A cluster is the equivalence class generated by "place s feeds transition t" — the connected components of the place-transition coupling — and with presets and postsets already stored as sorted slices, a union-find computes the partition cheaply. That single quotient unlocks two results at once. It yields the cluster count c that the Rank Theorem needs — and paired with the exact-rational rank routine from Principle 2, the theorem becomes a polynomial, certificate-bearing decision of simultaneous liveness-and-boundedness (well-formed iff rank C = c − 1), replacing the `is_covered_by_s_components` stub that today returns a hardcoded `false`. And it yields the S- and T-component decompositions the literature module already documents as if they existed. The word *cluster* appears exactly once in the crate — in a doc-comment transcribing the Rank Theorem — with no construction beneath it. The quotient is also used later: it is the partition composition requires at the capstone.

## Principle 4: The epistemic law

*Compute the reason, not just the answer — a finite witness a skeptic can recheck. Then allow any heuristic to choose what to try, because only the witness is believed. "Cannot yet decide" is an accurate result, not a failure.*

This is the principle that explains *why* the other three are run. Principles 1–3 describe what petrivet builds; this one describes what a build is *for*. It has several aspects: the founding design question answered, the trust boundary, and the method underlying the whole construction.

### Refuse to enumerate — and the *reason* for the refusal is the decidability sweet spot

A basic place/transition net cannot test a place for emptiness — there is no zero-test — and that single limitation is *exactly* why its core questions remain decidable. Universality was traded for analyzability. The proof of the trade is one step away: adding an inhibitor arc, which is a zero-test, allows construction of a Minsky machine, regaining Turing-completeness and losing decidability at once. So every extension on the roadmap — weighted arcs, capacities, reset and transfer arcs, inhibitor arcs — is not a free feature but a deliberate purchase, each gain in expressive power paid for in a loss of what can be decided; the worst-case complexity is the price, and the operative question on real instances is a distinct object. The refusal to enumerate is deliberate: it keeps the analysis inside the fragment where reasons are cheaper than answers.

### A verdict is a witness, not a bit; the boolean is the residue

The library's central commitment is that an analysis result should not be a boolean. The verdicts carry genuinely different witnesses: a scalar token-sum for the state-machine case, a Parikh firing-count vector for the marked-graph case, a replayable firing sequence for the general case, a firing sequence *and* an ω-marking for coverability, a siphon/trap pair or its counterexample for Commoner. The shape of each proof is dictated by the theorem that earned it; this is the vocabulary of *certifying algorithms*, where a procedure returns not just an answer but a witness a skeptic can check while trusting nothing about the procedure.

*This is partly proposed rather than implemented:* today it is a consistent practice, not yet a formal calculus. The five properties use five different result shapes; the `Result<evidence, counterexample>` pattern that fits appears in only one place; and the proofs are inert — there is no `verify()`, no `Certificate` trait, nothing that re-runs the cheap check. Yet the data is already certificate-shaped and already serializable: a firing sequence checks by replay against the existing `is_enabled`/`fire`; a marking-equation proof checks by recomputing m₀ + C·σ from its stored Parikh vector; a siphon/trap pair checks by re-verifying the closure conditions; the Farkas dual, once extracted, checks by one dot product. Unifying these behind a single `Certificate::check` and a `Verdict<Proof, Disproof>` carrying an explicit `Inconclusive` would make every answer auditable offline by an external solver, a referee, or a future version of the tool — with no new theory, only the retaining and typing of evidence the code already touches. The payoff is that the trusted base shrinks to the checkers alone; with the checkers small and verified, trust is *concentrated* rather than diffuse.

### State only what can be shown: a claim is strongest when it states exactly what it is

The same discipline the verdicts apply to proofs, the project applies to its claims. A claim is strongest not when it is emphatic but when it states exactly what it is, and the gradation matters: what can be *seen* in the code, what is *inferred* from it, what is *imagined*, what is *believed*, and what is *promised*. Precision, not emphasis, removes the handholds for doubt. The discipline is visible in the verdicts themselves: Commoner is an *iff* on free-choice nets and merely *sufficient* off them, and the code marks the difference rather than overclaiming. The tool returns `Inconclusive` rather than guessing, and it is explicit that the unbounded problems are decidable-but-Ackermannian, with the frontier sitting one line behind the ω check. The one place this discipline currently fails is worth stating plainly: the unbounded-liveness path returns every transition as L0, the same value a genuinely dead transition receives, so "we don't know" and "provably dead" carry the same value — distinguished only by a sibling method-field that the boolean wrappers discard. Making that distinction type-enforced is the outstanding honesty repair the calculus requires. (The bibliography keeps the same discipline: where conventions diverge — the Primer's |P|×|T| incidence against Murata's transpose — the code records which it chose.)

### The certificate is the trust boundary: the heuristic chooses what to try, the proof decides what is believed

This answers the founding design question: *what kind of knowing should the tool produce — a proof, or a good guess?* The codebase's answer, embodied before it was stated, is **both, with a boundary between them.** Because every decider that concludes emits a checkable certificate, and because acceptance requires an actual certificate, the *order* in which deciders are tried cannot affect the answer — only the time to reach it.

*This is the farthest-reaching proposed extension, and it rests on the certifying-decider design above.* It means a heuristic — even a machine-learned policy, even an AlphaGo-style scheduler — can be placed in charge of choosing what to try, and it can be arbitrarily wrong, and the tool's verdicts remain sound: a bad guess wastes milliseconds before a real proof arrives, and a correct verdict is never accepted without its witness. This can be stated as a theorem — soundness is *independent of the selection policy*; the proof is that the policy influences only which deciders run and in what order, never the acceptance predicate, so a returned verdict is correct irrespective of the policy — with the corollary that the learner may be optimized for cost by *any* means, including reinforcement learning under a misspecified reward, while remaining wholly outside the trusted base. This is a property Go does not have: in AlphaGo a wrong value estimate at a leaf costs the game; here a leaf is a proof, and a wrong estimate costs milliseconds. It is strictly stronger than MuZero, whose learned dynamics model sits *inside* the trust boundary and is only empirically sound; here the learner is outside, and correctness is structural.

The accompanying caveat is that the trust boundary's strength is exactly the *fraction* of deciders that are certifying — and today the bare-boolean shortcuts, and especially the two `Some(false)` stubs (the marked-graph liveness arm and `is_covered_by_s_components`), are trusted-but-unchecked, one of them a latent bug. **The real soundness risk in the whole construction is those stubs, not the machine learning.** Making every fast decider certifying is therefore the enabling precondition, not a parallel nicety; the evidence calculus and the learned portfolio are one project, and its first step is small and concrete.

#### The portfolio made operational: the cascade as data, and a policy that learns the effective theory of hardness

To realize this, lift the hardcoded cascade — the `match self.class()` repeated across five sibling files — into data: a `Decider` trait declaring each decider's polarity (proves-yes, proves-no, or exact), its cost class, and its soundness domain, with the `analyze_*` methods becoming a driver that gathers the admissible deciders, runs them in cost order, and accepts the first certificate. The default schedule reproduces today's behavior exactly — but the schedule is now a *parameter*, and the seed of the learned version already exists as a single `if` in the MCC entry, where the structural-versus-default technique tags are a binary record of which of two deciders fired. The learned policy generalizes that one branch into a function of the full structural feature vector, and the mapping onto the AlphaGo components is exact: a policy network choosing which decider to attempt (narrowing breadth), a value network estimating cost-to-certificate (truncating depth), and the certificate-gated execution as the search — but with *real* rollouts and *verified* terminal rewards.

The reason it should *help*, and not merely be safe, has a rigorous basis: the worst case is untouched, but hardness *on a distribution* is a distinct object, and the effective-theory literature establishes that an intractable local-update substrate can possess a closed, predictive macro-theory when the macro-variables are *autonomous* under the dynamics (Israeli–Goldenfeld's commuting-diagram criterion) and *sufficient* for the target (the causal-states / information-bottleneck criterion). Applied to this setting, that is feature-design doctrine: the policy should consume aggregate structural descriptors — net class, strong-connectivity, invariant dimension, siphon/trap and component counts, NUPN unit-tree shape — that summarize behavior without tracking the micro-marking, with a feature earning its place by mutual information with the hardness label. In a literal and defensible sense, the policy is learning the *effective theory of verification hardness* over the net distribution. (This is the one place Joscha Bach's computational functionalism is load-bearing rather than decorative — objects as coarse-grained aggregate descriptions of a finer substrate — and it is borrowed precisely that far and no further.)

#### The rungs: rising ambition, constant soundness

The ambition climbs a ladder whose every rung shares one guarantee. **Rung 0** is today's system: the hardcoded per-class cascade, one instance-dependent branch, sound and leaving performance wins unrealized. **Rung 1** is a cost-sensitive empirical hardness ranker — gradient-boosted trees over the structural features, no deep learning — that predicts the fastest admissible decider and schedules accordingly; it trains in minutes on the contest corpus, is sound by the theorem, closes much of the gap between the single-best and virtual-best solver, and carries the caveat that *performance* is not automatically monotone (a confident wrong pick can be slow), mitigated by running the near-free shortcuts first, capping the first expensive pick with a deadline, and blending toward the hand-ordered schedule as a prior. **Rung 2** turns the static ranking into an adaptive, preemptive controller — a Pandora's-box index policy, then offline reinforcement learning from the harness logs — that learns *when to abandon* a diverging exploration and race the cheap deciders in parallel; it needs the one piece of infrastructure the core lacks, a cooperative cancellation token, and it dominates Rung 1 because it can always reproduce the static schedule. **Rung 3** is the most speculative: promote *structural reductions* to actions, so the net becomes a shrinking residual and the search is an AND/OR proof-tree over reduced problems — AlphaZero in form. The discipline that keeps it sound where MuZero is not: each reduction must itself be *certifying*, with a `lift` that maps a certificate on the reduced net back to one on the original, so even a *buggy* lift cannot break soundness — it produces a certificate that fails the original checker and the search backtracks. And the reduction library turns out to *be* the whole apparatus recast as moves: removing an implicit place is licensed by the discarded Farkas dual of Principle 2; agglomeration by the cluster/siphon structure of Principle 3; splitting into sub-problems by the decomposition of the capstone. The through-line is the certificate: difficulty rises across the four rungs while soundness stays constant — a fixed guarantee under a rising capability curve, with the certificate as the training label, so the whole system is self-labeling and no oracle is required.

### Truth is what survives a quotient: keep only what is invariant under the don't-cares

Beneath the law runs a method. Every structure in the system names a group of "don't-cares" and keeps only the invariant under it. The marking equation forgets firing *order* and keeps the Parikh image — and its necessity-but-not-sufficiency is *exactly* the information that quotient discards, which is why the refinement loop must add back what order-blindness lost.

#### The machine origin is a don't-care: cost lives in a torsor, fitness in the quotient

Absolute timings have no canonical origin — machine speed and load are an arbitrary additive shift on log-cost — so the only physical content is *differences*: the ranking among deciders and their log-ratios, per instance. The cost vectors form a bundle of torsors indexed by the net's features; a *schedule* (today's cascade, a future learned policy) is a choice of origin, a section of that bundle, recorded *alongside* the data and never baked into it. The resulting discipline is concrete: persist the raw fibres tagged with their run context, treat them as non-comparable across machines except through differential invariants, prefer machine-invariant counters (states explored, LP solves) over wall-time, and phrase every regression test as a differential assertion against a committed baseline section — so the test is portable to a slower CI machine precisely because it lives in the ratios. The ranker of Rung 1 learns a static section of this bundle; Rung 2 learns an adaptive one; and the cost-sensitive regret it minimizes is itself a difference, origin-free — the torsor-quotient quantity made into a training objective.

#### Observe, never act: the harness and the one-way dependency

The substrate that produces these differential measurements is a separate observability domain, and its single discipline is that the dependency points *only toward the core*: the observer depends on petrivet; nothing in petrivet depends on the observer; the core does not know it is being measured. It observes without certifying and without scheduling — it records *whether* a check passed, once such a check exists, but does not implement one, and it runs deciders to measure them without choosing them for production answers. It is at once a self-test (a soundness sentinel asserting every accepted verdict still agrees with the oracle, which is exactly what catches the trusted-but-wrong stubs) and the future training set (the certificate is the label). Phase one needs no core change at all, reading the public method tags; a later, optional, additive seam exposes the individual deciders for per-decider timing. The whole contribution is safe to add and easy to remove because it is a pure downstream observer, and it is deliberately separated from the core algorithm work.

### Specify the interface before implementing it: the blueprint is committed ahead of the implementation

The deepest fact about the whole construction is a single file. `literature.rs` contains no executable code — only the theorems of Murata, Best–Devillers, and Esparza, each doc-linked to the function that implements it. It binds a published theorem and a Rust function so they reference each other; it *is* the README's motto, "bringing Petri net theory and application together," enacted literally as a seam in the source. And it deep-links to a layer that does not yet exist — `compute_invariants`, an `Invariants` type, an `SComponent`, a `crate::model` carrying evidence types — names that resolve to nothing. This is not a flaw; it is the most legible fact in the repository, because it makes *future potential* answerable rather than projective. The gap between the names the file invokes and the names that compile is a precise, self-authored map of the planned-but-unimplemented: the architecture has written its intentions in advance. Every abstraction the planned system would need has a pointer in that file *today* — which is why the planned work is completion, not invention, and why the seams were cut with the next generality already in mind.

## Capstone: Φ_PN

*How much a net resists factoring as a product: the smallest gap, over every cut the structure allows, between the whole and its reassembled parts. The gap is the quantity; the cut is the witness; the residual is what no decomposition removes.*

Not a fifth principle but the fixpoint of the whole construction — the one quantity whose definition requires all four principles at once. Composition is the synthesis: the operation by which nets decompose and recompose, and where the question "how much does the whole exceed its parts?" can be asked precisely.

### The factorization residual: a minimum over a partition lattice

A net's behavior, for a chosen property, either *factors* over a decomposition — so the parts can be analyzed in isolation and their verdicts recombined — or it does not. **Φ_PN** measures the degree to which it does not: the minimum, over every structural cut the net allows, of a distance between the whole's verdict and the tensor of the parts' verdicts under that cut. Φ_PN = 0 certifies that the property is *compositionally reducible* — analysis can proceed unit by unit. Φ_PN > 0 means the property is *irreducible*, a genuinely global fact no decomposition recovers, and the minimizing cut names precisely which cross-component coupling defeats modularity. This is a mathematical definition, not a metaphor: a minimum, over a well-defined lattice, of a well-defined factorization residual, with a property-specific composition rule — verdict-join for booleans, direct-sum-plus-interface-correction for invariant spaces, max for place bounds. It is, in effect, what the policy of Principle 4 cannot compress: the policy learns the macro-structure of hardness that *can* be coarse-grained, and Φ_PN measures exactly the irreducible remainder that resists it.

### Convergence: Φ requires every principle

The reason it is the capstone and not a sixth list item is that computing it requires all four principles at once. It requires the **order engine** (2) to produce the global verdict; the **structural decomposition** — the cluster quotient and its S-components (3) — to generate the partition lattice when no hierarchy is given; the **linear-algebraic layer** (2 again) — the matrix completed into its kernel, the Farkas dual — to define the composition rule on invariant spaces and the interface correction; the **certificate calculus** (4) to give the distance a meaning, defining what it is for two verdicts to disagree and witnessing the disagreement; and the **decision portfolio** (4) to make each per-unit analysis cheap enough that minimizing over a lattice of partitions is tractable. Underneath all of it, the **local monoid** of markings (1) is what makes "the tensor of the parts" a well-defined object in the first place. Φ_PN is the point where the architecture converges — the quantity it is, in a latent sense, built to compute.

### Where the partitions come from, and the cut as a learned action

The decomposition lattice is not hypothetical. The repository already parses NUPN — Nested-Unit Petri Nets, the contest's hierarchical format — into a forest of units, each owning local places and naming child units, with a `unit_safe` flag asserting a one-token-per-unit invariant; the fixtures carry real, deep unit trees. And *no analysis reads any of it* — the hierarchy is flattened away at conversion, the `unit_safe` flag (a genuine, exploitable linear invariant) is never checked, and there is no composition operator anywhere. NUPN is, today, a decomposition oracle the analysis has not yet learned to consult. Cutting that tree at any antichain of units yields a whole lattice of candidate partitions — exactly the domain Φ_PN minimizes over. And the idea closes a loop with Principle 4: a decomposition is a *good move* in the Rung-3 planner exactly when the property factors over the cut, which is to say exactly when the local Φ is small — so the planner's value network is, in part, learning to predict Φ_PN, recognizing the cuts along which the net falls cleanly apart from the integrated cores where the exhaustive cost must be paid. The capstone observation becomes a live heuristic inside the search.

### The boundary to IIT

One boundary is drawn deliberately. This is the rigorous, factorization-residual *core* of the integrated-information idea and nothing more — a quantity and a witness measuring a net's failure to factor. It says something about nets and nothing about minds. Integrated Information Theory appears nowhere in the repository — no Φ, no cause-effect repertoire, no exclusion postulate — and the full apparatus would require a stochastic semantics this qualitative analyzer does not have; importing it would be inventing structure rather than reflecting it. The leap from this residual to consciousness is exactly the part that is metaphor, and it is not taken here. What remains is precise: the quantity the whole architecture was built to converge on, measuring how much of a system survives every attempt to decompose it.

## The four principles, summarized

> **1 — SUBSTRATE.** A transition affects only its preset and its postset; the net is one matrix, C = Post − Pre. The whole's hardness is not localized in any single transition.
>
> **2 — COMPLETION.** Rather than walk the state space, complete it. Omega is infinity made into a type; the kernel is the matrix completed into its conservation laws. A finite witness stands in for the infinite fact.
>
> **3 — STRUCTURAL CLASS.** Choose by shape before computing. One closure answers a question about every subset at once; on free-choice nets the over-approximations become exact.
>
> **4 — EPISTEMIC LAW.** Compute the reason, not just the answer — a finite witness anyone can recheck. Then let a heuristic choose what to try; only the checked witness is believed. "Cannot yet decide" is an accurate result, not a failure.
>
> **Φ_PN.** How much a net resists factoring as a product: the smallest gap, over every cut the structure allows, between the whole and its reassembled parts. The gap is the quantity; the cut is the witness; the residual is what no decomposition removes.

Read in sequence: locality makes the question hard; completion makes it answerable without walking the trajectory; the right structural shape makes whole classes of it exact; and the certificate makes it safe to reach for those answers with a heuristic and still be correct — converging on the single quantity the architecture was built to compute.

*Provenance, stated explicitly: Principles 1–3, and the proof-carrying, decidability, and claim-honesty aspects of Principle 4, describe the implemented code — verifiable line by line, down to the two `Some(false)` stubs and the L0 hazard. The WSTS lift, the extracted Farkas certificate, the unified certificate calculus, the cluster quotient, the learned certifying portfolio and its ladder, the differential-measurement harness, and Φ_PN are proposed rather than implemented — offered, not asserted. The blueprint for nearly all of it is already in `literature.rs`.*
