# The Latent Architecture of `petrivet`

> Status: design analysis, reconciled to the ratified architecture. This document distinguishes three categories throughout: *implemented* (present and executable in the codebase today), *specified but not implemented* (named in documentation or referenced by dangling links, but not built), and *proposed/speculative* (an extension argued for here, grounded in the existing decomposition mathematics but not part of the project's stated scope). The core theory is Michael's; this document is an analysis of the codebase's structure and the architecture its interfaces were designed to receive. It supersedes an earlier draft that read the codebase as six co-equal abstractions converging on a single scalar; the falsifiability pass that ratified the architecture (recorded in the [foundations backlog](../foundations/foundations-backlog.md) and [foundational-design.md](../foundations/foundational-design.md)) inverted that emphasis, and this revision adopts the inversion.

## 1. One line, and everything that rests on it

There is a single line of code this entire project is a bet on. It does not exist yet. It is the driver's acceptance guard:

```rust
if certificate.check(original_net, m0, query) {
    return verdict;
}
```

Everything `petrivet` aspires to be — a fast analyzer, a certifying one, a learned one, eventually a compositional one — collapses to one property of that line: **`check` looks at nothing but `(original_net, query, verdict, certificate)`.** Not at which decider ran. Not at what structural class the net was filed under. Not at what reductions were applied on the way. Not at the policy that chose the order. A verdict becomes trustworthy at the instant an independent re-derivation, reading only the original problem and the proffered witness, says *yes*; and it becomes trustworthy for no other reason.

State the property so it can be killed. The falsifier of "soundness is independent of the selection policy" is a single counterexample: any decider whose verdict the checker accepts on evidence the checker did not actually re-establish against the original net. If such a decider exists, the firewall is breached and every downstream claim — the coverage headline, the safety of learned scheduling, the lifting of certificates across reductions — loses its warrant at once. The whole architecture is the discipline required to keep that counterexample from existing.

This is why the line is the subject of this essay, and why the rest of the structure is read in its light. The codebase today is a fast analyzer *without* the guard: the dispatch in [`analyze_reachability`](../../petrivet/src/api/system/reachability.rs) returns `UnreachabilityProof::MarkingEquationNoRationalSolution` directly from an `.is_none()` test (line 177), with no `check` between the solver and the caller. The verdict is trusted because the procedure is trusted. The ratified work is to interpose the guard — to make the procedure *untrusted* and the witness *checked* — and to discover, in doing so, that almost every structure already in the code was shaped to pass through it.

A note on what changed in the ratification, because this document once argued otherwise. The earlier reading treated the certificate, the coverage claim, the firewall, and the integration residual Φ as four faces of one thing, with Φ as the convergence point "the architecture is structured to compute." That ordering is now reversed. **The certificate-and-checker is the stone.** The empirical claim — that a polynomial structural tier decides a large, characterizable fraction of the real corpus, and abstains honestly elsewhere — is the headline. The policy-independence of soundness is the *enabling property* that makes both safe, not itself the contribution: as a theorem it is a one-line corollary of certifying algorithms (McConnell–Mehlhorn–Näher) composed with algorithm selection (Rice; SATzilla); its non-trivial content is the precondition the code must first discharge. Learned selection and Φ are sequel and horizon. This essay foregrounds the stone and fences the horizon.

## 2. The role of `literature.rs`

One file describes what `petrivet` intends to be more directly than any struct, algorithm, or benchmark. It is [`literature.rs`](../../petrivet/src/literature.rs), a Rust module that contains no executable code, only doc-comments — a citation index mapping theorems of Murata, Best–Devillers, and Esparza onto the functions intended to implement them. It deep-links to a module layer that does not yet exist. It references `structural::compute_invariants`, an `Invariants` type, an `SComponent`, and a `crate::model` carrying `SNetLivenessEvidence` and `TNetLivenessEvidence` — names that resolve to nothing in [`lib.rs`](../../petrivet/src/lib.rs); the `#[cfg(doc)] use crate::model` at line 409 is a broken intra-doc link. The documentation was written ahead of the implementation.

This is not a defect; it is the most informative property of the codebase. The gap between the names `literature.rs` invokes and the names that compile is a precise, author-supplied map of what is planned but not built — the project's self-authored blueprint. This document reads that blueprint in the order the ratified architecture imposes: first the trust boundary (the stone), then the structural generators that feed it (the headline's coverage), then the order and composition work (the horizon).

A note on scope. The thesis document in [`typst/masters`](../../typst/masters/thesis.typ) is at present a TUM template; the technical content lives in the code and in `literature.rs`, not in the `.typ` files. The structural-analysis region is roughly two-thirds specified and one-third implemented. Invariants are cited but not computed. Integrated Information Theory (IIT) appears nowhere in the repository — no Φ, no irreducibility, no partition in the relevant sense. The IIT material is fenced out of this essay entirely and deferred to [`the-factorization-residual.md`](the-factorization-residual.md), where it is developed only in its factorization-residual form and explicitly separated from any claim about cognition. Statements of the form "the code does X" are verifiable against the codebase at a `file:line`. Statements of the form "the code could become Y" describe proposed extensions anchored to an existing `todo`, stub, or dangling doc-link.

## Part I — The Trust Boundary (the stone)

### 3. The verdict calculus: one shape, with an inhabited *Inconclusive*

The codebase reflects a deliberate commitment: an analysis result should not be a boolean. The verdicts carry witnesses. [`ReachabilityProof`](../../petrivet/src/api/system/reachability.rs) is a four-way enum whose variants carry different witnesses — a scalar token sum for the live state-machine case (`LiveStateMachineTokenConservation`), a *Parikh firing-count vector* for the marked-graph case (`LiveMarkedGraphMarkingEquationIntegerSolution`), a replayable *firing sequence* for the general case (`FiringSequence`). [`CoverabilityProof`](../../petrivet/src/api/system/coverability.rs) carries both a firing sequence and an ω-marking that dominates the target. The Commoner/Hack result carries its siphon/trap pairs as positive evidence or its unmarked siphon as a counterexample. This is the model of *certifying algorithms*: a procedure returns not only an answer but a witness an independent checker can verify without trusting the procedure.

At present this is a tendency, not a calculus, and the gaps are exactly where the firewall leaks. The five properties take five different shapes — a three-arm enum, a two-arm enum, a `Result`, a `{values, method}` struct, a bare `bool`. There is no shared `Verdict` abstraction. The proof objects are *inert*: there is no `check()` anywhere, no `Certificate` trait, nothing that re-runs the cheap test. A caller handed `FiringSequence([t0, t1, t2])` must trust that the sequence fires; it cannot ask the proof to validate itself.

Two hazards in this region are not cosmetic. First, the L0 collision: for unbounded nets [`analyze_liveness`](../../petrivet/src/api/system/liveness.rs) returns *every transition as L0* — and L0 is also the value a genuinely dead transition receives. "We don't know" and "provably dead" are distinguished only by a sibling `LivenessMethod` field that the boolean convenience wrappers discard. Second, the unwitnessed positive: [`is_covered_by_s_components`](../../petrivet/src/api/net/mod.rs) returns a hardcoded `false` with a bare `// todo` (line 270–272), consumed for live free-choice nets in the boundedness path, and the marked-graph liveness arm returns `Some(false) // todo`. Both report a *definite* negative where they have computed nothing — the fast layer understating, in trusted code, what it has not earned the right to say.

The ratified target resolves all of this with two types and one constructor. The verdict becomes generic with a *type-distinct* inconclusive:

```rust
enum Verdict<P, N> {
    Proven(P),
    Refuted(N),
    Inconclusive,
}
```

`Inconclusive` is its own inhabitant, not a sentinel value of `Refuted` and not an `L0`. This single move kills the L0/"unknown" collision by construction: there is no longer a value that means both "provably dead" and "not yet decided," because the absence of a proof is a different variant from the presence of a disproof. Its falsifier is concrete: a property whose three outcomes do not fit "proven / refuted / don't-know" — and Petri-net analysis offers none, because every property here is a decision question whose honest abstention is exactly the third state.

The certificate becomes a trait with a single method:

```rust
trait Certificate {
    fn check(&self, net: &DenseNet, m0: &Marking, query: &Query) -> bool;
}
```

The `query` argument is not optional, and its presence is the design's sharpest commitment. A firing-sequence witness is *meaningless without its target*: a sequence `[t0, t1, t2]` certifies nothing until you say what marking it was supposed to reach. The witness and the question it answers are a single object; separating them admits a certificate that "checks" against the wrong query, which is no check at all. A property with no query argument — structural boundedness, say — passes a unit `Query`, and pays nothing for the uniformity.

The falsifier of the single-method trait is precise and worth stating, because the earlier draft asserted the trait without naming what would refute it: *a single-method `Certificate` with no associated types covers all five analyses — falsified by an analysis whose witness `check` cannot type.* Run it against the five witnesses the code holds. A `FiringSequence` checks by replaying against the existing `is_enabled`/`fire` machinery and comparing the final marking to the query. A marking-equation Parikh vector checks by recomputing `m₀ + C·σ` and comparing to the target. A siphon/trap pair checks by re-evaluating the closure conditions the code already tests (§7). A Farkas P-invariant (§5) checks by a single exact dot product. An ω-marking coverability witness checks by replaying to the covering marking — though here the falsifier *bites*: the emitted ω-witness is currently incomplete, lacking the pumping lasso the checker needs to certify the *un*bounded direction, and the marked-graph liveness witness carries no circuit-token data. The trait holds; two of its payloads must be enriched before their checkers are total. That is a finding, not a refutation: the witness set, not the trait shape, is what needs work.

Three properties make the calculus more than tidiness. The witnesses are already **owned, borrow-free, serializable** data — no closures, no references into the net. This is what lets a certificate be the training label for a learned selector *and* the input to an external checker *and* the payload of an interchange format, all at once. The constructor `accept` becomes the *only* public way to build `Proven`/`Refuted`: if `Verdict::Proven` can be constructed solely by passing a certificate through its checker, then policy-independence of soundness is a *type invariant*, not a runtime hope. And the trusted computing base shrinks to an enumerable set: the checkers, the exact-arithmetic kernel they call (§5), and the engine primitives (`fire`, `is_enabled`, the product order). Everything else — every decider, every policy, every future reduction, every learned model — sits outside it, where an error costs time and not correctness. ([foundational-design.md](../foundations/foundational-design.md) §3 fixes this boundary and proposes a CI lint asserting the dependency arrow never crosses it inward.)

None of this requires new theory. It requires retaining and typing the evidence the code already handles, and interposing the guard of §1 between the procedure and the caller.

### 4. The decider lattice, and why the order can be learned

`petrivet` is a *portfolio solver*, though it is not yet structured as one. For each property it runs an ascending-cost cascade of partial deciders. For reachability ([`analyze_reachability`](../../petrivet/src/api/system/reachability.rs)): trivial equality, then state-machine token conservation, then a live-marked-graph integer marking equation, then a rational LP filter, then an integer ILP filter, then full state-space exploration — each gated by structural class, each tried only if the cheaper ones abstained. This cascade is implemented and tuned, and it is the operational meaning of "try the tractable region first."

It is also entirely hardcoded — a `match self.class()` repeated across sibling files, the cost ordering living implicitly in the top-to-bottom order of statements inside each `analyze_*` method. There is no `Decider` trait. The metadata that ought to drive the schedule lives only in doc-comments: each decider's **polarity** (can it only prove YES, only prove NO, or decide exactly?), its **cost class**, its **soundness domain** (the marking equation is exact for state machines by total unimodularity; CHC is exact for free-choice liveness; both are one-sided elsewhere). Readable by humans; invisible to code.

The ratified shape lifts the cascade into data, and it makes a deliberate separation the earlier draft elided. The certificate calculus of §3 is *one* trait; the decision metadata is a *separate* one:

```rust
trait Decider {
    fn polarity(&self) -> Polarity;      // ProveYes | ProveNo | Exact
    fn cost_class(&self) -> CostClass;
    fn admissible(&self, features: &Features) -> bool;
    fn run(&self, net, query, budget) -> Outcome<Verdict<P, N>>;
}
```

A `Certificate` checks a witness; a `Decider` produces candidates and declares the metadata a scheduler reasons over. Conflating them — as "a certifying decider is just a checker that also runs" — loses the distinction between *what is trusted* (the checker, in the TCB) and *what is scheduled* (the decider, outside it). They are kept apart precisely so the firewall can run between them. The single learnable seam is `Policy::next`: given the admissible deciders and the features, choose which to run. The default policy reproduces today's hand-coded order exactly.

**The certificate is the trust boundary that makes the order learnable.** Because every accepted verdict carries a witness re-checked against the original net (§1), and because each decider is sound on its declared domain, the *order* in which deciders run cannot change the answer — only the time to reach it. A machine-learned policy can therefore schedule freely: a SATzilla-style selector over cheap structural features the code already computes — net class, place and transition counts, strong connectivity. It can be arbitrarily wrong and the verdicts stay sound, because a wrong guess wastes time before falling through to the exhaustive method, and a correct verdict is never accepted without its witness. Learning handles scheduling; the certificate handles soundness; the two are decoupled. This is how a heuristic — including a neural network — sits inside a soundness-critical verifier safely.

But the firewall protects only the *certifying* fraction. A decider that returns a bare `bool` is, in effect, part of the trusted base — and the two `Some(false)` stubs of §3 are exactly that: trusted code asserting a negative it has not proven. So the one hard prerequisite, the project's stated first move, is to demote those stubs to honest abstention (return `Inconclusive`, escalate) before any policy is allowed to vary. The figure of merit is then the **certifying fraction `f`**: the share of accepted verdicts that carry a checked certificate. Driving `f` toward one, and keeping the bare-boolean trusted set non-increasing under CI, is what gives the firewall its measured strength. The earlier draft's framing — "a learned, parallel, certificate-gated portfolio" — survives intact; the ratification only insists that the certifying property be made *structural and measured* first, and that selection be named for what it is: the SATzilla/Rice sequel, not the spine.

The anytime corollary follows once a cancellation token exists (it does not today; the only early exits are the ω short-circuit and process-level `catch_unwind`). With cooperative cancellation threaded into the exploration and solver loops, the cheap deciders can be raced, first certificate winning, the rest cancelled — cancellation changing only *when* a decider stops, never *what* is accepted.

### 5. Exact arithmetic as a soundness precondition, not a substrate

The linear-algebraic semantics is the second source of certificates, and it is where the firewall has a hole the earlier draft understated. A Petri net carries an incidence matrix C = Post − Pre, and the marking equation m = m₀ + C·σ is a necessary condition for reachability: if no nonnegative σ solves it, the target is unreachable. The kernel of Cᵀ — the P-invariants, the place-weightings y with yᵀC = 0 — are conservation laws, each bounding a weighted token sum for all time. Where the LP is *infeasible*, Farkas's lemma hands back a dual certificate: a place-weighting y that is an actual P-invariant witnessing the contradiction, y·C = 0 with y·(m' − m₀) ≠ 0. A checkable, human-readable conservation law that *explains* the unreachability.

`petrivet`'s realization is deliberately thin, and the thinness is the problem. The only algebraic object is [`IncidenceMatrix`](../../petrivet/src/core/analysis/incidence.rs): a dense `Box<[i32]>` with exactly two methods, `new` and `get`. It cannot multiply, transpose, or rank. There is no rational matrix type, no Gaussian elimination, no `num-rational` in the tree. P- and T-invariants are computed nowhere; they exist only as prose and the dangling links to `structural::compute_invariants`. Everything algebraic is delegated to [`semi_decision`](../../petrivet/src/core/analysis/semi_decision.rs), which builds the marking equation row by row into `good_lp` constraints and hands them to `microlp` — over `f64`. Look at the assembly: each coefficient is `f64::from(incidence.get(p, t))` (line 128); the integer variant rounds via `v.round() as u32` (line 104); the structural-boundedness witness is an `f64` weight vector (line 270). The library does not perform linear algebra; it phrases linear algebra as floating-point feasibility and queries an LP.

This is the hole, and it is two holes. The earlier draft framed the loss as merely *the discarded Farkas dual* — the solver computes the dual at the infeasible boundary and the code throws it away, returning a payload-free `MarkingEquationNoRationalSolution`. That framing is correct but incomplete, and [foundational-design.md](../foundations/foundational-design.md) §1 names the rest. **First**, the dual is unsound to emit *verbatim*: a floating-point weight vector is not a proof; a checker re-running `y·C` over `f64` could accept a y that is not exactly a P-invariant. **Second, and worse,** the *negative verdict itself* rests on `microlp`. `Unreachable` is returned when the floating-point simplex *fails* to find a rational solution ([reachability.rs](../../petrivet/src/api/system/reachability.rs) line 177). A spurious "infeasible" near a degenerate vertex — a feasible rational system the f64 solver declares empty — yields a silent **false `Unreachable`**. This is worse than the `Some(false)` stubs of §3, because it is data-dependent, invisible, and *the firewall does not catch it*: there is no positive object to check on the negative path. The witness the checker would re-derive is the very thing the solver got wrong.

So exact arithmetic is not, as the essays once framed it, primarily the enabler of *new* algebraic results. It is the precondition for the *existing* deciders to be certifying at all. The required core is classical: a fraction-free (Bareiss) exact-rational kernel over the existing dense `IncidenceMatrix`, giving `rank`, `nullspace_basis` (the P/T-semiflows — the Colom–Silva minimal generators `compute_invariants` was named to deliver), and exact Farkas-dual extraction, with the law that every returned object is verifiable by one exact dot product. With it, two things become possible that no amount of f64 can reach by rounding. The negative reachability verdict carries an *exact* separating invariant, re-checked in ℚ before the verdict returns — `y·C = 0 ∧ y·(m'−m₀) ≠ 0` in exact arithmetic, or, equivalently, an exact null-space membership test of `m'−m₀` in `ker(Cᵀ)`. And `rank(C)` arrives — the missing ingredient for the Rank Theorem of §6. The floating LP is retained, but demoted: an inexact *filter* that may route and may abstain, but never constructs `Proven` or `Refuted`. The exact kernel is what crosses into the TCB.

Both semantics are completions, and now the asymmetry is sharp. The order side (§6, §8) completes the reachability relation upward into ideals, with ω as the limit, and its witnesses are already exact (token counts are integers). The algebra side completes the incidence matrix into its kernel, with Farkas duality as the certificate — but its witnesses are floating, and so its certificates are, today, *unsound*. Building the exact kernel does not add a feature; it closes the firewall on the half of the verdicts that currently leak through it.

## Part II — The Tractable Region (the headline's coverage)

### 6. Structural deciders, born certifying

There is a region of Petri net theory where the two over-approximations become exact. For *free-choice* nets, liveness is decided exactly, in polynomial time, by a purely structural condition — Commoner's theorem — and boundedness by the rank of the incidence matrix against the number of clusters. This is the Desel–Esparza tractable region, and it is the substance behind the README's promise to "intelligently choose the most efficient algorithm for your net's structure." It is also the headline claim's substance: the *characterizable fraction* of the real corpus that a polynomial structural certifier decides without ever touching the state space.

What is implemented here is real and is the most notable technique in the repository. Siphons and traps — the structural duals governing token starvation and trapping — are computed by [`maximal_siphon_in`](../../petrivet/src/core/analysis/siphon_trap.rs) and `maximal_trap_in`, exact De Morgan duals: the same shrinking procedure ("while some place is bad, remove it") under mirror-image conditions. They are closure operators — each computes the unique maximal siphon or trap inside a set. The Commoner/Hack criterion needs to know whether every minimal siphon contains a *marked trap*; the naive route enumerates all traps and tests containment, but `commoner_hack_criterion` instead enumerates minimal siphons and, for each, computes the maximal trap inside it — because "the siphon contains a marked trap" iff "the maximal trap in the siphon is marked." One closure answers an existential-over-subsets question in a single computation. The result is a [`Result<Box<[SiphonTrapPair]>, UnmarkedSiphonTrapPair>`](../../petrivet/src/api/system/chc.rs): every siphon paired with its marking witness, or the exact unmarked siphon that can starve — a deadlock certificate.

The implemented region stops abruptly, and the stop is informative. The word *cluster* appears exactly once in the crate — a doc-comment in [`class.rs`](../../petrivet/src/core/class.rs) transcribing the Rank Theorem: "the rank of the incidence matrix of N is equal to c − 1, where c is the number of clusters." There is no cluster construction. There is no rank function (§5). `is_covered_by_s_components` returns hardcoded `false`. The S-component decomposition, the Rank Theorem, and the whole `structural::` module are specified, named, and unbuilt.

The missing keystone is a single construction, and it is the cheapest high-leverage line in the algebraic core: **the cluster quotient.** A cluster is the equivalence class generated by "place s feeds transition t" — the connected components of the place-transition coupling. With presets and postsets already stored as `UniqueSortedSlice`s, one union-find computes the partition near-linearly. That single quotient unlocks both halves at once: the count c the Rank Theorem needs (which, paired with §5's exact rank, becomes a polynomial certificate of simultaneous liveness-and-boundedness, replacing the `false`-returning stub), and the S-/T-component decompositions — sub-nets analyzable in isolation, each itself a `DenseNet` so the existing analyses recurse.

Here is the build-order disagreement worth stating plainly, because it cuts against the intuitive order. The structural generators of this part (the backlog's Epic B) should *not* come first. The `Decider`/`Policy` refactor (backlog D1) should precede them, so that each generator is **born** as a registered, certifying `Decider` — declaring its polarity, its cost, its admissible domain, and emitting its certificate through the §3 calculus — rather than retrofitted into one later. A generator written first and registered second is a generator written against the wrong interface: it will return a bare verdict, or a witness shaped for its own convenience rather than for the checker, and the firewall will have to be re-threaded through it after the fact. Written second, against an existing registry, it has no choice but to be certifying from its first line. The sequencing is: discharge the soundness preconditions (the stubs, the exact kernel), land the verdict-and-certificate contract, land the decider registry, *then* grow the generators — each one measured against a structural-coverage floor that can be reported today, before any generator lands, by reading which tier fired from the existing `*Method`/`*Proof` tag.

### 7. Two traits, and two concrete types: a discernment

The earlier draft argued that four constructions recur across the codebase — an *order*, a *completion*, a *closure*, a *quotient* — and that making each one a trait would let the engine written once for markings operate wherever the structure recurs. The pattern is real. The conclusion was too generous, and the ratification narrows it.

A trait earns its keep when it has more than one implementor *and* something polymorphic consumes them. By that test, exactly two of the four earn a trait now.

**`Closure` earns it.** Siphon and trap are two genuine implementors — the shrinking loop instantiated with mirror-image conditions, duality realized as an incidence-direction flip — and the CHC criterion consumes both. A `Closure` trait with `maximal_siphon_in` / `maximal_trap_in` as instances deduplicates the two shrinking loops and names the duality. The real scaling risk it must confront is not the choice among engines but that minimal-siphon enumeration is worst-case exponential and sits on the CHC liveness path; the trait is the place to make the enumeration policy explicit (cap, or restrict to the subclass where the siphon count is bounded).

**`WellQuasiOrder` earns it** — with two implementors via the WSTS lift, developed in §8. The order is the consumed-by-many abstraction *par excellence*: the state-space engine is a single generic procedure, and the order is the one piece of it not yet abstracted. `Ideal<D>` generalizes `Omega`. Two implementors (ℕ and a second trivial WQO domain) exercise the seam.

The other two do **not** earn a trait, and saying why is the discernment. **`Quotient` (the cluster construction)** has, in the codebase as it will stand, a *population of one*: the cluster equivalence over places-and-transitions. There is no second quotient for a `Quotient` trait to range over. A trait abstracting a single concrete construction is not generality; it is aesthetics dressed as generality — an interface with one implementor, paying the indirection cost of polymorphism for none of its benefit. The cluster quotient should be a concrete type with a concrete method. **`Invariants`/`Completion` (the linear-algebraic completion into the kernel)** is the same: one completion, the null-space of C, with one exact implementation. The earlier draft's "make each of the four abstract" elided this; the falsifier of *its* claim is simply the implementor count. Two of the four constructions have two implementors and a polymorphic consumer; two have one and none. Abstract the first two; write the second two out concretely, and say so in the code.

This is not a retreat from the pattern — the pattern is the right lens for *reading* the architecture. It is a refusal to let a descriptive symmetry dictate the API. Generality is earned by a second instance and a polymorphic use, not asserted by a recurring shape.

## Part III — The Horizon

### 8. The order, lifted: WSTS and the unbounded frontier

The state-space machinery has a single implementation. The reachability graph and the coverability graph are not two algorithms; they are [one generic engine](../../petrivet/src/core/state_space/mod.rs) parameterized by `TokenOps`, the trait abstracting the per-place token scalar — its zero, its successor, its "at least one" test. There are exactly two implementors: `u32`, and [`Omega`](../../petrivet/src/core/state_space/coverability.rs), the enum `{ Finite(u32), Unbounded }` representing infinity as a type, with absorptive arithmetic (incrementing ω is a no-op, ω dominates every finite value). In this design the Karp–Miller coverability graph is the reachability graph run over ℕ ∪ {ω} instead of ℕ, and a single function makes it explicit: [`TryFrom<CoverabilityGraph> for ReachabilityGraph`](../../petrivet/src/api/state_space/coverability.rs) — an ω-free coverability graph *is* a reachability graph, recovered by unwrapping each `Finite(k)`.

This is a fragment of the theory of **Well-Structured Transition Systems** (Finkel; Abdulla; Finkel–Schnoebelen): any transition system with a well-quasi-order on its states under which transitions are monotone. For such systems coverability is decidable via the ω-acceleration the code performs in [`omega_accelerate`](../../petrivet/src/core/state_space/coverability.rs) — walk the ancestors, promote strictly-greater coordinates to ω — with Dickson's Lemma guaranteeing termination.

The order is the one un-abstracted element of the engine. It is the blanket `impl<T: Ord> PartialOrd for IdxMarking<T>` in [`marking.rs`](../../petrivet/src/core/marking.rs) — the componentwise product order, with a `merge_ordering` fold returning `None` as soon as two coordinates disagree in direction. A repository search for `WellQuasiOrder`, `WSTS`, `Ideal`, or `Dickson` returns nothing. The genericity that exists is over the *fiber* — the value in each place — not over the *order on states*. `Omega` is the *ideal completion* of (ℕ, ≤): the principal ideals ↓k together with the top ideal ℕ; the cross-type comparison the code performs (a concrete `u32` marking against an `Omega` marking) compares a state against an ideal. This is the Finkel–Goubault-Larrecq theory of ideal completions, implemented by hand for ℕ without being named.

Lifting `Omega` to `Ideal<D>` over an arbitrary WQO domain, the product order to a `WellQuasiOrder` trait (carrying the test-enforced wqo obligation that licenses termination), and acceleration to "join with the limit of the dominating chain" earns the second trait of §7. The far-horizon payoff is the WSTS zoo — the same explorer driving lossy channel systems (the Higman order on words), ν-Petri nets, broadcast protocols, BVASS — but that is recorded, not scheduled. The *near*-term payoff is sharper and is the reason the lift is worth doing now: it converts the blanket `Inconclusive` at the ω-frontier into a refinement loop. Today the boundary is one line — `if marking.has_omega() { return Inconclusive }` ([reachability.rs](../../petrivet/src/api/system/reachability.rs) line 195–196) — and these problems are decidable but Ackermannian (the Leroux–Schmitz frontier). The `// todo: backwards coverability` markers point at the Abdulla-style backward algorithm, defined over upward-closed sets and their finite bases of minimal elements, for which [`UniqueSortedSlice`](../../petrivet/src/core/unique_sorted_slice.rs) — with its O(n+m) subset and intersection scans — is the natural representation. An ideal-based forward refinement (expand-enlarge-check; Geeraerts–Raskin–Van Begin) would replace today's blanket abstention with a loop carrying a partial over-approximation certificate. The ω-marking `analyze_coverability` already returns is the starting over-approximation such a loop tightens — once enriched with the pumping lasso its checker needs (§3).

### 9. Composition, NUPN, and the residual deferred

Everything so far analyzes a single net. The final concern is how nets decompose and recombine, and it is where the codebase is most purely potential.

The repository parses **NUPN** — Nested-Unit Petri Nets, the Model Checking Contest's hierarchical format — into [`NupnStructure`](../../petrivet/src/api/pnml/nupn.rs): a forest of units, each owning local places and naming child units, rooted, with a `unit_safe` flag asserting a one-token-per-unit invariant. The fixtures contain real, deep unit trees. No analysis reads any of it: the hierarchy is flattened during conversion, the only consumers of the parsed NUPN are tests, and the `unit_safe` flag — a genuine, exploitable, *checkable* linear invariant — is never verified and never used. There is no composition operator: no `compose`, no gluing along shared interface places, no monoid, no category. NUPN today is a decomposition oracle the analysis never consults.

Two concrete, near-term items live here, and both feed the trust boundary rather than the horizon. The `unit_safe` flag should be surfaced as a *certificate* under the §3 calculus — a free, checkable safety/boundedness witness whose checker confirms the one-token-per-unit invariant against the original net. And the unit forest should survive conversion intact, because it is a candidate partition: cutting the tree at any antichain of units yields a lattice of partitions, which is the substrate any compositional analysis needs.

The compositional recombination itself — `compose` along shared-interface places — is recorded, not scheduled, and is narrower than the earlier draft implied. It is *not* a free tensor product. It is the Kronecker-descriptor "direct-sum-plus-interface-correction" the compositional school (open nets, Baldan et al.; Kronecker descriptors, Buchholz, Donatelli) already studies, where the entire difficulty lives in the interface correction. When it is built, it should be defined per property — boundedness and invariant spaces, where the correction has a precise meaning — and not for boolean verdicts, where "composition" is just assume-guarantee reasoning, a mature field that needs no new object here.

And the residual. The earlier draft built toward a single scalar Φ_PN — a minimum over the partition lattice of a distance between the whole's verdict and the product of the parts' verdicts — and presented it as the quantity "the architecture is structured to compute," the convergence of all six concerns, coincident with the mathematical kernel of integrated information. The ratification dissolves the scalar. The single net-level Φ_PN, the boolean-verdict Φ, and the "built to compute Φ / needs all four roots" necessity claim do not survive examination: the boolean version is assume-guarantee reasoning under a new name, and the necessity claim mistook a descriptive convergence for an architectural one. What survives is narrower and real — *per-property* factorization residuals (a boundedness residual, an invariant rank-defect residual) that are computable, monotone, theorem-backed-zero on the tractable classes, and whose deliverable is their *measurement over the corpus*, not their metaphysics. That treatment, and the IIT fence in full, belong to [`the-factorization-residual.md`](the-factorization-residual.md) and are developed there; this essay carries neither the scalar nor the "built to compute Φ" claim. The composition oracle (NUPN, the partition lattice) is the part that belongs *here*: a structural substrate, consulted at last, feeding certificates back into the boundary of Part I.

## 10. The architecture, in the ratified order

The structures, read in the order the inversion imposes — the stone first, the coverage it earns second, the horizon last:

- **`Verdict<P, N>` + `Certificate`** (§3) — three-valued, with an inhabited `Inconclusive` that kills the L0/"unknown" collision; a single-method `check(net, m0, query) -> bool` with the query *required*; owned, serializable witnesses; `accept` as the sole constructor of a positive verdict, making policy-independence a type invariant. *The stone.*
- **`Decider` + `Policy::next`** (§4) — the cascade as data, polarity and cost and admissibility as fields, the schedule as the one learnable seam, sound by the firewall — once the `Some(false)` stubs abstain and the certifying fraction `f` is measured and its complement non-increasing. *The enabling property, built and measured.*
- **An exact-rational core** (§5) — Bareiss rank, null-space, exact Farkas dual; the precondition that makes the *existing* marking-equation deciders certifying, and that closes the silent false-`Unreachable` hole f64 leaves open. *Not a substrate — a soundness precondition.*
- **The cluster quotient, and structural generators born as `Decider`s** (§6) — one union-find unlocking the Rank Theorem and the S-/T-components; the generators sequenced *after* the registry (D1 before Epic B) so each is certifying from its first line. *The coverage the headline claims.*
- **`Closure` and `WellQuasiOrder`** (§7–§8) — the only two of the four recurring constructions that earn a trait, each with two implementors and a polymorphic consumer; `Quotient` and `Invariants` stay concrete, a trait over a population of one being aesthetics, not generality. The WQO lift's near payoff is the backward-coverability refinement at the ω-frontier. *Earned generality, and the horizon.*
- **NUPN-as-partition** (§9) — the `unit_safe` flag surfaced as a certificate, the unit forest preserved as the partition lattice; the composition operator and the per-property residuals recorded, the scalar Φ dissolved and deferred to [`the-factorization-residual.md`](the-factorization-residual.md). *Recorded, not scheduled.*

Each has a doc-link, a `// todo`, a stub, or a documented name pointing at it today. That is what `literature.rs` provides: a specification of where the remaining structure goes. The future of `petrivet` is not a pivot or a rewrite but the construction of one line — the acceptance guard of §1 — and the discovery, in building everything that must stand behind it, that the codebase was shaped to receive it. The README's stated aim is "bringing Petri net theory and application together"; here that aim is realized concretely by `literature.rs`, where a published theorem and a Rust function reference each other, and by the trust boundary that turns a fast analyzer into a checkable one. The core work is Michael's; the central results belong to him. The point of this analysis is that the interfaces were designed one level of generality away from where the code rests — and that the level they were designed for is the level at which a verdict carries its own proof.
